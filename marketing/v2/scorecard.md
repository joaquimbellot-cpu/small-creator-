# Scorecard: Conversion Critic (version 2)

Read: product-brief.md (sections 2, 9, 10), ideas.md (all 16 concepts plus the sharpened copy), research.md (skimmed). Nothing from v1.

How I scored. Each of five things is out of 10. A 7 is ordinary. 40 of 50 is needed to survive, and any single "no" in the bland test kills a concept whatever it scores.

How I read bland-test line 1 ("offer visible in the first 2 seconds"): the viewer must be able to READ, with sound off, that this is a sponsor offer and that it is free to apply. "Free to apply" alone is not the offer: free to apply what? If the word "sponsor" is not on screen in seconds 0 to 2, that line is a no.

---

## 1. Verdict

1. One concept survives out of 16: L1 The Line, at 41 of 50. Fifteen die.
2. Four pass the bland test (L1, C4, U3, U6). Only L1 also clears 40. The next best is L2 at 34, then U3 and U1 at 32.
3. Pattern in the kills: 7 of the 8 "wild" concepts sell a metaphor (dolls, marbles, claw, loupe, jingle) and never put the word "sponsor" on screen in the first 2 seconds. A stranger sees a pretty object and learns nothing. Several are also hard to generate.
4. Pattern in the UGC set: five of the six are a presenter reading the brief's facts aloud (free, a person picks, you can say no). That is safe and it is bland. Safe is the very failure that sank the last campaign.
5. Deeper pattern: 14 of 16 answer an objection (too small, costs money, stuck with a sponsor). Only L1 sells the thing the viewer wants, which is saying "this video is sponsored by" to their own audience. Nobody taps Apply because an objection was answered. They tap because they want the thing.

## 3. Concept by concept

**L1 The Line (41).**
- Strongest: the only concept built on desire. The sentence every small YouTuber has imagined saying, cut off before the name, is a real thumb-stopper.
- Weakest: the first 6 seconds explain nothing, so the 5-second understanding test passes narrowly. The 1,000 to 50,000 qualifier does not appear until second 6.
- Honesty risk: medium to high. Rehearsing "This video is sponsored by" can read as "this person has a sponsor" or "you will get one". The script line "A person picks one for your channel" drops the "to suggest" the Copywriter added everywhere else. "Want to say this line?" leans toward promise.
- Production risk: low. One actor, one room, hard cuts. Watch lip-sync on the cut-off word.
- Sound-off: weak as written. The gag is the spoken line. It must be burned in as a caption, "This video is sponsored by ____".

**L2 This Big (34).**
- Strongest: a fast visual joke with a clear first beat (slap on the board, bell) and the low line is ownable.
- Weakest: it gives permission, not desire. "You're big enough" is relief. It also leans on the fairground sign, which any brand can borrow.
- Honesty risk: medium. "This big for a sponsor" can read as "a sponsor awaits at 1,000". The end-card line is required.
- Production risk: low to medium. Keep the board identical across clips.
- Sound-off: the arrow and "1,000 subs" read, but "sponsor" is only spoken. As written, that is why it fails line 1.

**U3 Right Size (32).**
- Strongest: clearest of the set. A person, a door frame, a range, and the offer in the first lower third.
- Weakest: "Too small?" makes the viewer feel small first. Desire is reassurance only. It is the same idea as L2 with a slower start.
- Honesty risk: low, if the Copywriter's "to suggest" version is used, not the first script's "picks a sponsor for your channel".
- Production risk: low to medium. Magnifying glass, spread arms. Pencil marks must be added in post.
- Sound-off: works if the lower third and the bracket graphic carry it.

**U1 Five Fingers (32).**
- Strongest: the best clarity in the set. The offer is spoken and written at once, and the five-item application makes "apply" feel small. Best CTA.
- Weakest: it is a presenter explaining a form. Swap the logo and a competitor could run it.
- Honesty risk: low.
- Production risk: HIGH. Counting five fingers, one per beat, is the single most likely thing for AI to get wrong. Hands and fingers are in 4 of the 6 shots.
- Sound-off: works. The pop-ups carry it.

**U6 Step Right (31).**
- Strongest: the quiz is a different shape from the rest, and the viewer self-qualifies.
- Weakest: the viewer cannot step anywhere. "Step right" is a floor game aimed at a thumb. 23 seconds with 42 words is long.
- Honesty risk: low to medium. "Three yeses? Apply" with YES ticks reads as an approval check. The Copywriter flagged it.
- Production risk: medium. Floor tape, YES/NO text must be added, three fingers shown twice.
- Sound-off: works with graphics.

**C4 Big Red Button (30).**
- Strongest: red button, boing, first-person hands. Simple and easy to produce. It passes the bland test.
- Weakest: it opens on refusing. The viewer sees sponsors being thrown away and never sees why they would want one. Desire is control, which is only a comfort.
- Honesty risk: medium to high. A belt of sponsor boxes rolling toward one creator is "several sponsors queuing", which is on the risk list. The green press nudging a box forward implies a deal in progress.
- Production risk: low to medium. Hands on buttons, belt stop on cue.
- Sound-off: works. The boing is lost, the text carries.

**C2 Boomerang (30).**
- Strongest: the best pure hook of the wild set. The bonk is funny and relatable in one second.
- Weakest: relief from a chore is not desire. Hook text has no "sponsor", and the joke is shared with U2, C7 and H2.
- Honesty risk: low to medium. A grinning creator filming at the end, with a presenter thumbs-up, reads as "it worked".
- Production risk: medium. Paper-plane loop physics, hands, a second actor.
- Sound-off: the pratfall reads, the "bonk" is lost.

**C1 Height Check (29).**
- Strongest: the most ownable image. A doll parade under a carnival bar is a distinct picture.
- Weakest: wooden dolls passing a bar say "you pass a test" and nothing about a sponsor. The word "sponsor" arrives in VO at 5 to 10 s, and it says "sponsor idea".
- Honesty risk: medium. Passing the bar with a ding reads as approval.
- Production risk: medium to high. Keeping the same dolls across clips, plus a real hand set against them.
- Sound-off: mostly works. The number band carries it.

**C5 Tiny Studio (28).**
- Strongest: miniature plus giant loupe is a genuine scroll-stopper and "a person looks" is a strong, honest idea.
- Weakest: the word "sponsor" does not appear until 10 s. Four seconds in, the viewer has seen a toy room and nothing else.
- Honesty risk: medium. The giant hand placing a gift box in the corner reads as "a sponsor delivered".
- Production risk: medium. Loupe distortion, the partial eye, scale.
- Sound-off: weak. The picture needs the words.

**C8 Hand-Picked Claw (27).**
- Strongest: a claw machine "switched off" is a good twist.
- Weakest: a claw machine is a prize game. It says "win", which is the wrong message and works against "a person picks by hand".
- Honesty risk: high. A box lifted from the machine is a win. Prize imagery implies a result.
- Production risk: high. Glass reflections, claw physics, hand-box contact.
- Sound-off: partial. The power-down is lost.

**C3 Two Marbles (26).**
- Strongest: a satisfying marble run is a real hook for ASMR viewers, and the idea is unique.
- Weakest: explaining terms with marbles and a toll arm is a riddle. The cost message is lost on anyone who needs it explained. A toll arm also means paying.
- Honesty risk: low to medium. The toll arm reads as a charge.
- Production risk: high. Marble physics, split path, composite.
- Sound-off: the click-clack is the hook and it is lost.

**U2 Sent. (26).**
- Strongest: the silent refresh and the deadpan turn are a good comic beat.
- Weakest: the joke lands at 7 to 11 s. The first 4 seconds are a presenter announcing the offer, so the skit starts late. The category's default joke.
- Honesty risk: low.
- Production risk: medium. Leaf-drop physics, typing hands, laptop back to camera.
- Sound-off: partial. The read-aloud email is lost, the silent beat survives.

**U5 Two Ways (25).**
- Strongest: the same woman in both halves isolates the only difference, which is the way.
- Weakest: a before/after comparison is stock. "Pitching: you write every pitch" goes a little beyond section 2.
- Honesty risk: low to medium. The relaxed "applying" half implies a result.
- Production risk: HIGH. Split screen plus holding one person and one desk identical in two states.
- Sound-off: works.

**U4 Three Spots (24).**
- Strongest: props for each reason (tag, magnifier, pushed cushion) are well matched to the brief.
- Weakest: "three reasons" is three promises and the most generic UGC format there is.
- Honesty risk: low. A cushion labelled "sponsor" sliding toward the presenter shows a sponsor arriving, then being pushed away.
- Production risk: high. Three locations and five clips with a teal-haired person who must stay identical.
- Sound-off: works.

**C7 Two Desks (22).**
- Strongest: the messy desk is recognisably the viewer's own.
- Weakest: chaos vs calm is every before/after ad ever made. It repeats C2 and U5.
- Honesty risk: medium. The calm creator filming happily is an implied result.
- Production risk: HIGH. Split screen of two matching desks, then a wipe.
- Sound-off: works.

**C6 The Jingle (19).**
- Strongest: the chant repeats the offer.
- Weakest: the word "sponsor" never appears anywhere in the ad, not even on the end card. The viewer is told "free to apply" three times and never what to.
- Honesty risk: low.
- Production risk: low for video, but the music has to be original.
- Sound-off: fails. The chant is the ad.

## 4. Concepts scoring 34 to 39: the one change

**L2 This Big (34).** Cannot be saved by one change. The bland fail is a one-line text fix (print "You must be this big for a sponsor" on the sign in the first frame). The six-point gap is desire: reassurance is not wanting. The low line is worth keeping as a static ad and as a qualifier. It does not carry a video.

---

## 5. Survivor: L1 The Line

- **Must not be lost in production:** the line is never finished. The name is cut off every time, including in the last shot, and no brand and no "got it" moment appears anywhere.
- **Fix before it is sketched:** replace "A person picks one for your channel" with "A person picks a sponsor to suggest". As written it breaks the "to suggest" rule on the one concept that most needs it. Two smaller fixes are on the same pass: burn the line in as a caption "This video is sponsored by ____" for sound-off, and move the 1,000 to 50,000 chip to seconds 2 to 3 so the 5-second test is not marginal.

---

## 6. Overlaps (which is stronger)

- **No-pitching cluster: C2, U2, C7, U5.** All say "stop cold-emailing". C2 is stronger (30 against 26, 22, 25), because its hook is a one-second gag. Keep C2 and drop the other three.
- **Height cluster: L2, U3, C1.** All are a measuring device with the range on it. L2 is stronger (34), then U3 (32), then C1 (29). L2 is not a merger of C1 and U3, it is the best of them. The team leader's claim that L2 "fixes" the weakness holds for the joke but not for desire.
- **A person picks by hand: C5 and C8.** C5 is stronger (28 against 27), with lower honesty risk and easier production.
- **Terms only: C3 and C6.** C3 is stronger (26 against 19), though neither explains the offer.
- **Presenter reading facts: U1, U4, U3.** U1 is stronger (32 against 24). U3 holds up because of its prop.
- **Control: C4 and H4.** Same idea. Keep C4.
- **L1 overlaps nothing.** It is the only desire concept.

---

## 7. Top 5 ranked

1. **L1 The Line (41).** It shows the sentence they dream of saying and then tells them they can apply for it, free.
2. **L2 This Big (34).** It tells them, with a joke, that the line is low, so a channel of 1,000 feels allowed to apply (needs "sponsor" printed on the sign).
3. **U3 Right Size (32).** A real person marks their range on a wall, which kills "too small" in a few seconds.
4. **U1 Five Fingers (32).** Five items on a hand make the application look small and the CTA feels earned (needs a fix for the finger counting).
5. **U6 Step Right (31).** Two quick questions let the right viewer qualify themselves, and the "Apply" lands as the answer to them.

Note for the team: only the first of these clears 40. Concepts 2 to 5 are near misses and should not be produced as they stand.
