# Angles for the Smallfold creator offer (v2)

Source: marketing/v2/product-brief.md only. Sections 2 and 9 govern every line.

The customer psychology below is my own reasoning about how small creators tend to think. It is not research and it has no numbers.

The offer in one line: **A sponsor chosen for your small YouTube channel. Free to apply. You pay only if a deal is done.**

---

## 1. Desires (what they would say to a friend)

1. "I just want a brand to say my channel is good enough."
2. "I'm tired of feeling too small for anything."
3. "I want someone else to do the sponsor hunting. I'd rather be filming."
4. "I want a sponsor that actually fits my videos."
5. "I want to try this without risking anything."
6. "I want to know someone real looked at my channel."

## 2. Fears and frustrations (same voice)

1. "Every sponsor thing seems to be for big channels."
2. "Cold emails make me cringe, and nobody writes back."
3. "I don't want to pay for something that goes nowhere."
4. "What if I get stuck with a brand my viewers hate?"
5. "It's probably just a bot or a list I'll get lost on."
6. "I don't have time for forms, calls and back-and-forth."

---

## 3. Objections and the fact that kills each

| Objection | Killing fact (section 2) | How the ad handles it |
|---|---|---|
| "My channel is too small." | It is for channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers. | Put the number on screen. The viewer sees their own size named. |
| "It will cost me money." | Joining is free. No deal, no commission. | Say "free to apply" first. Say "no deal, no commission" second. |
| "I'll be stuck with a bad fit." | You can turn down any sponsor. Nothing goes ahead without your yes. | Show the thumb pushing away. Say it plainly. |
| "It's another list or bot." | A person looks at your channel and chooses by hand. No algorithm. | Say "a person". Say "no algorithm". |
| "It'll eat my time." | One short application. You do not look for sponsors yourself. | Say "apply once". Never list the steps. |
| "Will I definitely get a sponsor?" | No fact kills this. The honest answer is no. | Never imply it. The end card carries "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." Sell the chance, not the result. |
| "I'm under 1,000 subscribers." | No fact kills this. They cannot join yet. | The qualifier tells them up front. Do not tease or hint. Accept that they leave. |
| "Is this for platforms other than YouTube?" | No fact kills this. It is YouTube only, for now. | Say "YouTube channels" in the qualifier. |
| "What will it take from my fee?" | Only a partial fact. Commission is charged only once a deal is done and comes out of your fee. The rates are not confirmed. | Say only "no deal, no commission". Show no rates or amounts until the owner confirms. |
| "Is this real? Who are these people?" | No fact kills this. There are no testimonials and no results to show. | Do not fake trust. Be plain about the terms. Point to the real apply page. Never use a reviewer, a rating or a "creators love it" line. |
| "What do they want from me?" | The application is five things: name, email, channel link, what it is about, subscriber count. Phone is optional. | Say "a short application" in body copy. Do not list the five things in hooks. |

## 4. Five selling angles

**Proof, for all five:** facts only. There are no testimonials, no results, no numbers of creators or deals, and no named brands. Every angle rests on the terms of the offer and nothing else.

### Angle 1: The Number

- **Promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply, and a person looks at your channel to choose a sponsor to suggest. (Always paired with: applying doesn't promise one.)
- **Emotion:** Recognition. "That's me. I'm not too small."
- **Proof available:** The size range in section 2. The "by hand, a person" fact. No results.
- **Opening lines (offer on screen at 0 seconds):**
  1. "1,000 to 50,000 subscribers? Apply." (5 words)
  2. "Small channel? This sponsor offer is for you." (8)
  3. "1,000 to 50,000 subscribers: free to apply." (7)
  4. "Made for channels your size. Free to apply." (8)
- **Visual stand-in:** The big, bright "1,000 to 50,000" number over a daylight desk and ring light.
- **Kills:** "My channel is too small."

### Angle 2: No Pitching

- **Promise:** Apply once, and you never have to write a pitch email to a sponsor yourself.
- **Emotion:** Relief. The cringe is gone.
- **Proof available:** "You do not look for sponsors yourself." "One short application." No results.
- **Opening lines:**
  1. "Skip the pitch. Apply once." (5)
  2. "No cold emails. One short application." (6)
  3. "Stop pitching sponsors. Apply once instead." (6)
  4. "Sponsor offer: no pitching, free to apply." (7)
- **Visual stand-in:** A messy, real filming corner, creator filming happily. A hand sweeps a laptop shut. Text is added in the edit.
- **Kills:** "It'll eat my time."

### Angle 3: Free to Try

- **Promise:** It's free to apply, and you pay only if a deal is done.
- **Emotion:** Safety. "Why not?"
- **Proof available:** "Joining is free." "No deal, no commission. Commission comes out of your fee, only once a deal is done." No rates, no amounts.
- **Opening lines:**
  1. "Free to apply. No deal, no commission." (7)
  2. "Apply free. Pay only if a deal's done." (8)
  3. "Sponsor offer. Free to apply. Zero upfront." (7)
  4. "Free to apply. Seriously, nothing upfront." (6)
- **Visual stand-in:** A price tag that reads 0. An empty wallet that stays closed. A receipt with nothing on it. Text added in the edit.
- **Kills:** "It will cost me money."

### Angle 4: Your Yes

- **Promise:** You can turn down any sponsor, and nothing goes ahead without your yes.
- **Emotion:** Control. Pride in the channel.
- **Proof available:** "You can turn down any sponsor. Nothing goes ahead without your yes." No results.
- **Opening lines:**
  1. "You can say no to any sponsor." (7)
  2. "Nothing happens without your yes. Free to apply." (8)
  3. "Your channel. Your yes. Free to apply." (7)
  4. "Turn down any sponsor you want." (6)
- **Visual stand-in:** The moment of choice: a thumb on a tick, then a hand pushing something away.
- **Kills:** "I'll be stuck with a bad fit."

### Angle 5: A Real Person

- **Promise:** A person, not an algorithm, looks at your channel and chooses a sponsor to suggest.
- **Emotion:** Being seen. "Someone actually looked."
- **Proof available:** "A person looks at your channel and chooses." "By hand. No automatic list, no algorithm." No results.
- **Opening lines:**
  1. "A person picks a sponsor for your channel." (8)
  2. "No algorithm. A person looks at your channel." (8)
  3. "Someone actually looks at your channel. Apply." (7)
  4. "Hand-picked sponsor idea for your small channel." (7)
- **Visual stand-in:** A bright desk with a creator's channel-style grid on a phone on a stand. The screen is blank or blurred, and the real text is added in the edit. (No readable screens in generated shots.)
- **Kills:** "It's another list or bot."

## 5. The offer stack (order to say it)

**In 5 seconds**
1. Who: "1,000 to 50,000 subscribers."
2. What: "A sponsor chosen for your channel."
3. Price: "Free to apply."
4. Action: "Apply."

**In 10 seconds**
1. Who: "For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers."
2. What: "A person looks at your channel and picks a sponsor to suggest."
3. Control: "Say no to any sponsor."
4. Price: "Free to apply. No deal, no commission."
5. Action: "Apply."

**In one static image**
1. Top, largest: "1,000 to 50,000" with "YouTube subscribers" under it.
2. Headline: "A sponsor chosen for your small channel."
3. One line: "Free to apply. No deal, no commission."
4. Small line: "You can say no to any sponsor."
5. Button: "Apply".
6. Footer: logo from the real file. "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor."

## 6. Call to action

Only one: **Apply.**

**Five ways to say it**
1. "Apply."
2. "Apply free."
3. "Tap Apply."
4. "Apply with your channel link."
5. "Apply. It's one short application."

**Supporting line:** "Free to apply. No deal, no commission. For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers."

**The end card must have**
- The big "1,000 to 50,000" number.
- "Free to apply. No deal, no commission."
- The "Apply" button, placed from a real file.
- The link: smallfold.netlify.app/apply
- The real logo, placed on top (never generated).
- "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor."
- Nothing else. No second call to action, no deadline, no discount.

The owner switches on each platform's AI-content label.

## 8. What to bet on

**Bet first: Angle 1, The Number.**
- The number is the offer's packshot. It is the fastest way to make the right person stop.
- It names the viewer's own size, so it kills the biggest objection in the first second: "I'm too small."
- It filters. People outside the range scroll on, which is fine.
- It is a true, plain fact. It does not lean on a promise.

**Test against it:**
1. **Angle 3, Free to Try.** The strongest rival. "Free" and "no deal, no commission" speak to fear of cost. It may win with viewers who already know they fit.
2. **Angle 2, No Pitching.** It targets a different pain: the cringe of cold emails. It may win with creators who have tried pitching and hated it.

Angles 4 and 5 are best kept as body-copy support lines and as a second round if the first three are close.

---

## Check against section 9

- Every promise says a person "looks at" or "picks a sponsor to suggest". None says you will get one.
- The only outcome language is "apply" and "free to apply".
- No money amounts or commission rates. The rates and the "2 working days" line stay out until the owner confirms them.
- No testimonials, no ratings, no customer voice. Desire and fear lines in sections 1 and 2 are for the writer's use only. They must never appear as something a customer said about Smallfold.
- No creator claims a result or reviews Smallfold. Any AI person only speaks to the viewer about the offer.
- No company story, no mission, no urgency, no other company named.
