# Ideas added by the team leader

The brainstorm stayed careful and nobody used the one picture every small YouTuber already has in their head: the moment they say "this video is sponsored by". These two concepts go into the pool and are scored by the Critic like all the others.

---

## L1. The Line

**One-line pitch:** A small creator rehearses "This video is sponsored by..." over and over, cut off before the name every time, then tells you how to apply for a sponsor, free.

- **Angle:** 1, The Number, carried by the desire itself.
- **ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Format:** Rehearsal skit turning into a presenter talking to the viewer. Obviously acted. The person never says they have a sponsor, never names a brand, never mentions the service helping them.

**On camera:** Invented person in their early 20s, bright bedroom studio in daylight, ring light, camera on a tripod, coloured wall. Label "AI actor" on screen throughout.

**Outline**

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Tight on the creator, mid-gesture to their own camera, big grin. Smash cut before the name. | "This video is sponsored by" then a hard cut and a pop. | Top: "Want to say this line?" Chip: "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." |
| 2-6 s | Three fast retakes of the same line: announcer voice, whisper, pointing with both hands. Each cut off before the name. | "Sponsored by" (pop). "Sponsored by" (pop). "Sponsored by" (pop). A light beat builds. | "Take 2" / "Take 3" / "Take 4" as small counters. |
| 6-12 s | The creator stops, turns to the phone camera, drops the act and talks straight to the viewer. | "Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? You can apply for a sponsor. It's free." | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" then "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 12-17 s | Same framing, counting off with small nods (no finger counting). | "A person picks one for your channel. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | "A person picks." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Sting, then silence. | "Apply" button. "Free to apply. For 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script, word for word (41 words, about 18 seconds of speech):**
"This video is sponsored by. Sponsored by. Sponsored by. Sponsored by. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? You can apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks one for your channel. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why a small YouTuber stops:** it is the sentence they have imagined saying. Hearing it cut off four times is funny and itchy.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the ad names their size and says the thing they want is something they can apply for today, at no cost.
- **Not stock, not a rival's:** the cut-off line is a repeatable device that belongs to this offer. It also gives endless hook variants (new deliveries of the line).
- **Honesty check:** nobody claims a sponsor. The line is never finished, no brand is named, and the end card carries "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor."
- **AI difficulty:** easy. One person, one bright room, short lines, hard cuts that hide joins.
- **Static version:** the creator mid-gesture, big type "This video is sponsored by ______", under it "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply free."

## L2. This Big

**One-line pitch:** A fairground "you must be this big" sign with the line set absurdly low: 1,000 subscribers.

- **Angle:** 1, The Number. Kills "my channel is too small".
- **ONE promise:** Your channel is big enough to apply: the offer starts at 1,000 subscribers.
- **Format:** One presenter, one prop, one joke. It merges the two "height" ideas from the brainstorm (C1 and U3) and fixes their weak point: a bar you walk under says "too short", while a low line you tower over says "big enough".

**On camera:** Invented presenter in their 30s, bright outdoor daylight or a sunny studio wall, next to a tall candy-striped measuring board. The board is blank in the generated footage; the line and the numbers are added as graphics.

**Outline**

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Presenter slaps the striped board at knee height. | Slap, fairground bell. "You must be this big for a sponsor." | Arrow at knee height: "1,000 subs". Chip: "Free to apply." |
| 2-6 s | Presenter stands up straight beside the board; the line is far below their shoulder. They look down at it, then at the lens. | "One thousand subscribers. That's the line." | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" |
| 6-12 s | Presenter to camera, close. | "A person looks at your channel and picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no." | "A person picks." / "You can say no." |
| 12-16 s | Presenter taps the board twice and points down to the button. | "Free to apply. No deal, no commission." | "Free to apply." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Bell. | "Apply" button. "For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script, word for word (38 words, about 17 seconds):**
"You must be this big for a sponsor. One thousand subscribers. That's the line. A person looks at your channel and picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. Free to apply. No deal, no commission."

- **Why a small YouTuber stops:** everyone knows the fairground sign, and the low line is a visual punchline about their own channel.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the thing stopping them ("too small") is answered before they think it.
- **Not stock, not a rival's:** the low line is a single owned image and works as a thumbnail and a static.
- **Honesty check:** "this big for a sponsor" describes who can apply. The end card carries the disclaimer.
- **AI difficulty:** easy to medium. Risk: keeping the board the same between shots. Mitigation: one location, two clips, graphics added after.
- **Static version:** the striped board with the low line, "You must be this big: 1,000 subs", "Free to apply", Apply button.
