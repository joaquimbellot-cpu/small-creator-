# Round 2: ideas added by the team leader

Round 1 taught one thing: sell the wanting. These three go into the round 2 pool and are scored like the rest.

---

## L3. Missing Sponsor

**One-line pitch:** A creator does a full, sincere sponsor read while holding up nothing at all, then notices their empty hands.

- **ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Desire, in the creator's words:** "I already know exactly how I'd do a sponsor segment. I just don't have the sponsor."
- **On camera:** Invented cooking creator in their 30s, bright kitchen, daylight, phone on a tripod. Label "AI actor" throughout.

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Creator beams at the camera and presents an invisible product in both open hands, like a prize. | "Huge thanks to today's sponsor." | Title: "Sponsor segment." Stamp: "Missing: the sponsor." |
| 2-6 s | They turn the nothing over, tap it, hold it to the light. Completely sincere. | "Fits right in the kitchen. Comes in three colours." | Caption of the spoken line. Chip: "Free to apply." |
| 6-8 s | They look down at their empty hands. Beat. They look back up at the lens. | Silence, one small pop. | "Something missing?" |
| 8-14 s | Straight to camera, relaxed. | "Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free." | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" then "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 14-17 s | Same, small shrug. | "You can say no to any of them. No deal, no commission." | "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Sting. | "Apply" button. "For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (38 words):** "Huge thanks to today's sponsor. Fits right in the kitchen. Comes in three colours. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free. You can say no to any of them. No deal, no commission."

- **Why they stop:** a sponsor read with no product is instantly odd, and every small creator has practised one in their head.
- **Why they tap:** the joke names the exact gap in their channel, then says the gap is something they can apply to fill.
- **Competitor test:** the "missing sponsor" stamp and the empty hands only make sense for an offer that supplies the sponsor.
- **Sound off:** "Sponsor segment. Missing: the sponsor. Free to apply." reads in the first 3 seconds.
- **Honesty:** the creator has no sponsor, which is the whole joke. No brand, no result, no claim about the service. The made-up praise is aimed at thin air, not at Smallfold.
- **AI difficulty:** easy to medium. Risk: open hands in close-up. Mitigation: medium shot, hands below chest, cut on the look down.
- **Static:** creator presenting empty hands, "Sponsor segment. Missing: the sponsor.", "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply free."
- **Hook family:** the invisible product changes with the niche (gaming, woodwork, gardening), which gives cheap variants.

## L4. Next First

**One-line pitch:** A shelf of a small creator's milestones, each with a little handmade trophy, and one stand left empty: first sponsor.

- **ONE promise:** After your first 1,000 subscribers, the next first is one you can apply for, free.
- **Desire, in the creator's words:** "First video, first comment, first thousand. I want the next one."
- **On camera:** No presenter. A sunny shelf in a creator's room, small handmade trophies (a clay play-shape with no logo, a paper star, a tiny gold cup), one empty stand. A hand enters. Warm voice-over. Label "Made with AI".

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Fast slide along the shelf, stopping hard on the empty stand in a patch of sun. | Three quick dings, then a hollow knock. | Labels over the trophies: "First video" "First comment" "First 1,000 subs". Over the empty stand: "First sponsor: ____" |
| 2-5 s | A hand dusts the empty stand and straightens it. | Voice: "One spot left on the shelf." | "Free to apply." joins the empty-stand label. |
| 5-10 s | Push in on the empty stand; the trophies blur behind it. | Voice: "Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? You can apply for a sponsor. It's free." | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" then "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 10-14 s | The hand sets down a plain blank card on the empty stand, leaning, like a place-holder. | Voice: "A person picks one to suggest. You can say no." | "A person picks." / "You can say no." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Soft ding. | "Apply" button. "No deal, no commission." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (31 words):** "One spot left on the shelf. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? You can apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks one to suggest. You can say no."

- **Why they stop:** they read "First 1,000 subs" and recognise their own shelf; the blank is an itch.
- **Why they tap:** it frames a sponsor as the next milestone of a channel their size, not a prize for big channels.
- **Competitor test:** it only works for an offer that starts at 1,000 subscribers.
- **Sound off:** fully readable; it is the strongest muted concept and the strongest static.
- **Honesty:** the stand stays empty. Nothing is won. The blank card is a place-holder, not a result.
- **AI difficulty:** easy. No faces, one location, slow hands. Labels are graphics.
- **Static:** the shelf with three labelled trophies and the empty stand, "First sponsor: ____", "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply free."

## L5. Not 100K

**One-line pitch:** A creator shoves a giant "100K" off the screen and a small, solid "1K" drops in: that is where you can apply for a sponsor.

- **ONE promise:** You do not need 100,000 subscribers to apply for a sponsor. You need 1,000.
- **Desire, in the creator's words:** "I thought sponsors were for later. I want it now, at the size I am."
- **On camera:** Invented gaming or tech creator in their 20s, bright room, coloured wall. The numbers are big 3D-looking graphics added afterwards; the actor only mimes pushing and catching. Label "AI actor".

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Creator leans a shoulder into something huge off-frame and heaves. | Grunt, heavy scrape. "Sponsor at a hundred K?" | Giant "100K" filling the frame, sliding out. Title: "Sponsor at 100K?" |
| 2-4 s | They dust their hands; something small drops in from above and they catch it at chest height. | Thud, bright ding. "Try one K." | "1K" lands. Chip: "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 4-10 s | To camera, holding the invisible small number like a ball. | "If you've got one thousand to fifty thousand subs, you can apply for a sponsor now. It's free." | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" |
| 10-15 s | They toss it gently toward the lens. | "A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | "A person picks." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Sting. | "Apply" button. "For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (40 words):** "Sponsor at a hundred K? Try one K. If you've got one thousand to fifty thousand subs, you can apply for a sponsor now. It's free. A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why they stop:** the number they believe in is thrown off the screen in the first second.
- **Why they tap:** "later" becomes "now".
- **Competitor test:** it needs an offer that really starts at 1,000.
- **Sound off:** "Sponsor at 100K?" then "1K. Apply for a sponsor. Free." reads muted.
- **Honesty:** it states who can apply. "100K" is the viewer's own assumption, shown as a question, not a claim about anyone else.
- **AI difficulty:** medium. Risk: miming against graphics that are added later. Mitigation: simple push and catch, graphics timed to the motion.
- **Static:** "100K" crossed out by hand, "1K" beside it, "Apply for a sponsor. Free."
