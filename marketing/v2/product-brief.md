# Product brief: the Smallfold creator offer (version 2)

**Date:** 23 November 2026. **Status:** working brief, confirmed by the owner's "yes" to the interview summary.

This file is the only source of product facts for version 2. If a claim is not in section 2, it does not go into an ad. Version 1 (`marketing/v1/`) is archived; none of its concepts, hooks, scripts or pictures are reused.

## 1. The product in one line

**A sponsor chosen for your small YouTube channel. Free to apply. You pay only if a deal is done.**

There is no physical product and no buy button. The "product" is this offer, and the "buy" click is **Apply** at https://smallfold.netlify.app/apply.

## 3. Who buys

People who run a YouTube channel with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers. They film, edit and upload on their own. Many have never had a sponsor.

How they think about sponsors is the Strategist's work (`angles.md`). That is reasoning, not fact, and no ad may present it as research or a statistic.

## 4. Top 3 benefits (what the customer gets)

1. **A sponsor for a channel your size.** You do not need to be big first. The offer is built for 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers.
2. **No pitching, no cold emails.** You apply once. Finding the sponsor is not your job.
3. **Nothing to lose.** Free to join, nothing to pay unless a deal is done, and you can say no to any sponsor.

## 5. What makes it different

- It is only for small channels.
- A person chooses the sponsor for your channel. It is not a marketplace you have to browse or a list you get dumped on.
- You never pay up front.

Say these as benefits to the viewer. Do not name or mock any other company, platform or kind of creator.

## 6. Objections that stop people applying

| Objection | The fact that answers it |
|---|---|
| "My channel is too small." | It is for channels from 1,000 subscribers |
| "It will cost me money." | Joining is free. No deal, no commission |
| "I will be stuck with a sponsor that does not fit." | You can turn down any sponsor |
| "It is another list or bot." | A person looks at your channel. No algorithm |
| "It will take a lot of my time." | One short application. You do not look for sponsors |

## 7. Price and offer

- **Lead offer (use everywhere):** Free to apply. No deal, no commission.
- **Call to action (the only one):** Apply.
- **Qualifier (use often, it makes viewers self-select):** For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers.
- No discounts, no deadline, no limited places, no bonus. None exist, so none may be shown.

## 8. What looks great on camera

A service has no packshot. These are the visual stand-ins that make the offer concrete and desirable. The owner chose "all generated", so nothing here is real footage.

- **The subscriber number.** A big, bright "1,000 to 50,000" is the fastest way to make the right viewer stop. It is the offer's packshot.
- **The creator's world, bright:** daylight desk, ring light, camera on a tripod, a phone on a stand, a messy and real-looking filming corner. Version 1 was dark, slow and silent; version 2 is bright, quick and has sound.
- **The moment of saying yes or no:** a thumb, a tick, a hand pushing something away. It shows control.
- **"Free" and "0":** a price tag that reads 0, an empty wallet that stays closed, a receipt with nothing on it. Text is added in the edit, never generated.
- **The dream scene, as a dramatisation:** a small creator filming a sponsored segment. Allowed only as an obviously acted scene with no real brand, no claim that it happened, and no line where the person says Smallfold got them a sponsor.

**Screens and text:** AI garbles screens and letters. Generated shots never show a readable screen, a logo or lettering. The offer text, the Apply button and the logo are placed on top from real files.

## 9. Hard rules for every concept

- Only the facts in section 2. No numbers of creators, sponsors or deals. No earnings or money amounts. No testimonials, ratings or reviews. No named brand or creator.
- Never "you will get a sponsor", "guaranteed", or anything that reads that way.
- AI-generated people may talk to the viewer about the offer. They never pose as a customer, never review Smallfold, never describe a result.
- No fake urgency: no countdowns, "almost full", "last places".
- No company story, founder story, mission or values. Talk about the offer and what the viewer gets.
- No other company's logo (including YouTube's), no copyrighted music or characters, no real person's face or voice.
- The owner publishes and switches on each platform's AI-content label.

## 10. The bland test (from the owner)

A concept dies if any of these is false:

1. The offer is on screen in the first 2 seconds. (For a service, "product visible" means the offer is visible.)
2. Anyone understands what it is and why to apply within 5 seconds.
3. It makes one specific promise, not vague words.
4. It would not look like a stock ad or a competitor's ad.
5. It never talks about the company.
