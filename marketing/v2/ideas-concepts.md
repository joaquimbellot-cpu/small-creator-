# Ideas and concepts for the Smallfold creator offer (v2)

Sources: `product-brief.md` (sections 2, 9, 10), `angles.md`, `research.md` only.

The offer: **A sponsor chosen for your small YouTube channel. Free to apply. You pay only if a deal is done.** One call to action: **Apply.**

## Look and sound rules for everything below

- **Bright.** Daylight, saturated candy colours (yellow, coral, mint, electric blue), clean white or pastel backgrounds. No dark grading anywhere.
- **Fast.** A new shot every 1 to 2 seconds in the first 5 seconds. One clear action per shot.
- **Loud.** Every concept is designed around sound: a bold sound effect on the very first frame, a beat or jingle under it, and a signature "ding" or "thunk" on every key word. Sound-off still works because every key word is also on screen.
- **Text and logos are added after.** All words, numbers, ticks on screens, the Apply button, the link and the logo are graphics placed on top. Nothing readable is generated in the scene. Stand-in objects are blank (blank cards, blank boxes, unlabelled buttons).
- **Persistent offer chip.** From second 0 to 2, the words on screen carry the offer ("Free to apply"). From about second 2 to 3, a small bottom chip adds "For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers". The chip is not counted in the hook word limits below.
- **Standard end card** (used by every concept, 3 s): the big "1,000 to 50,000"; "Free to apply. No deal, no commission."; the Apply button (real file); smallfold.netlify.app/apply; the real logo (real file); small line "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." Sting plus final ding. Nothing else.
- **Every ad is labelled** with the platform's AI-content label by the owner.

# PART 1: Hooks (15)

Each hook is the first 2 seconds. "Words" is the on-screen text, 7 words or fewer. "Spoken" is 8 words or fewer. The offer ("Free to apply" or its twin "no deal, no commission") is on screen inside these 2 seconds in every hook.

**H1. Desk Bell Slam** (Angle 1, The Number)
- Picture: bright daylight desk, ring light glowing. A hand slaps a chrome desk bell. On the ding, two huge glossy graphic numbers slam in with a camera shake.
- Words: "1,000 to 50,000 subs. Free to apply." (7)
- Spoken: "Small channel? This one's for you." (6)

**H2. Slam the Laptop** (Angle 2, No Pitching)
- Picture: extreme close-up. A hand slams a laptop shut. A burst of white paper planes explodes out of the gap.
- Words: "No cold emails. Free to apply." (6)
- Spoken: "Close the laptop." (3)

**H3. The Stubborn Wallet** (Angle 3, Free to Try)
- Picture: a fat, shiny leather wallet on a desk. A hand pulls at it and it stays shut with a loud lock click. A price-tag graphic swings in showing 0.
- Words: "Free to apply. No deal, no commission." (7)
- Spoken: "Wallet stays shut." (3)

**H4. Flick It Away** (Angle 4, Your Yes)
- Picture: first-person view. Plain pastel gift boxes slide toward the camera on a belt. A giant finger flicks one off the edge with a cartoon boing.
- Words: "Turn down any sponsor. Free to apply." (7)
- Spoken: "Your channel. Your yes." (4)

**H5. Giant Magnifier** (Angle 5, A Real Person)
- Picture: scale trick. A miniature filming corner sits on a table. A huge brass magnifying glass swings in and the tiny corner fills the lens.
- Words: "Chosen by a person. Free to apply." (7)
- Spoken: "No algorithm. A person." (4)

**H6. Made to Measure** (Angle 5)
- Picture: a yellow tailor's tape snaps tight around a toy-size creator desk, with a puff of chalk dust. A tailor's hand marks it.
- Words: "Tailored to your channel. Free to apply." (7)
- Spoken: "Made to measure." (3)

**H7. Finger Snap Presenter** (Angle 2)
- Picture: a bright presenter on a lemon-yellow wall, already mid-sentence, snaps their fingers. Behind them a tower of paper planes collapses.
- Words: "Skip the pitch. Free to apply." (6)
- Spoken: "Stop pitching sponsors. Seriously." (4)

**H8. The Yes Stamp** (Angle 4)
- Picture: an oversized rubber stamp thumps down on a blank pastel card. The thud is huge. A green tick appears (added in the edit).
- Words: "Your yes first. Free to apply." (6)
- Spoken: "Nothing moves without your yes." (5)

**H9. Zero Tag** (Angle 3)
- Picture: a giant blank price tag swings into frame like a game-show wheel stopping. A graphic 0 lands on it with a ka-ching-style "pop" (a pop, not a coin sound).
- Words: "Apply free. Pay only on a deal." (7)
- Spoken: "Nothing upfront." (2)

**H10. Lamp in a Jar** (Angle 1)
- Picture: tilt-shift miniature creator desk inside a glass jar. A giant hand clicks on a tiny desk lamp. The jar glows warm gold.
- Words: "Small channel? Free to apply." (5)
- Spoken: "Small is welcome." (3)

**H11. Boomerang Planes** (Angle 2)
- Picture: a creator throws a paper plane. It loops back and bonks them on the forehead. Slide-whistle sound.
- Words: "Done pitching? Apply free." (4)
- Spoken: "Done with cold emails?" (4)

**H12. Fit Board** (Angle 1)
- Picture: a carnival-style height board with a bright coloured band. A wooden doll with a tiny ring light on its head slides under the bar. Ding.
- Words: "For 1,000 to 50,000 subs. Apply free." (7)
- Spoken: "You fit? Apply." (3)

**H13. Tiny Megaphone** (Angle 4)
- Picture: a presenter leans into the lens and whisper-shouts into a tiny orange megaphone. A confetti-free, plain-colour pop-up behind them.
- Words: "Sponsor picked for you. Free to apply." (7)
- Spoken: "Someone picks. You decide." (4)

**H14. Drop the Net** (Angle 2)
- Picture: a creator rummages through a bush with a comically huge butterfly net, then drops it and grabs a camera. The camera lands with a satisfying click.
- Words: "Skip sponsor hunting. Free to apply." (6)
- Spoken: "Drop the net. Keep filming." (5)

**H15. Switch It Off** (Angle 5)
- Picture: a hand flips a big toggle switch to OFF. A machine hum dies. A person with a mug leans toward a laptop-free desk and looks.
- Words: "No algorithm. Free to apply." (5)
- Spoken: "Switch it off." (3)

## C1. Height Check

**One-line pitch:** A parade of little creator dolls walks under a carnival height bar. Each one clears it with a ding.

- **Angle:** 1, The Number.
- **ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply, free.

**Outline**

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Bright carnival board with a bold coloured band. First doll (wooden, with a tiny ring light) slides under the bar. | Desk-bell ding, drum-roll tick. | "For 1,000 to 50,000 subs. Apply free." |
| 2-5 s | Fast cuts: a doll with a mic, a doll with a tripod, a doll with a headset. Each passes under the bar. Each gets a different ding pitch. | Rising ding scale, a bouncy marimba beat. | The number band glows: "1,000 to 50,000 subscribers" |
| 5-10 s | Camera pulls back to the whole row of dolls. A real hand sets a doll on the bar, then lifts it and holds it toward the lens. | Presenter voice-over (warm, quick): "Your channel's this size? A person picks a sponsor idea. You can say no." | "A person picks. You can say no." |
| 10-15 s | Close on the bar and the band lighting up. Bar lifts. | Final ding, beat drops out. | "Free to apply. No deal, no commission." |
| End card | Standard. | Sting. | Standard. |

- **Why a small YouTuber stops:** the exact number of their own channel size is the hero. The parade of dolls is hypnotic, fast and silly.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the board tells them they fit. The ad kills "my channel is too small" before they think it.
- **Not stock, not a rival's:** a carnival-ride measurement board with wooden dolls is a unique, ownable image. No other sponsor offer uses height checks.
- **AI difficulty:** medium. **Main risk:** doll consistency across clips and a clean bar. Mitigation: same doll design in every prompt, band colour added in post.
- **Static version:** a row of wooden dolls under a bright height bar, the big "1,000 to 50,000 subscribers" over the band, "Free to apply" under it, Apply button.

## C2. Boomerang

**One-line pitch:** A creator's paper-plane cold emails keep flying back at them, until they stop throwing.

- **Angle:** 2, No Pitching.
- **ONE promise:** Apply once, and you never write a pitch email to a sponsor yourself.

**Outline**

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Creator at a bright desk throws a paper plane. It loops back and bonks them. | Slide whistle, bonk. | "No cold emails. Free to apply." |
| 2-5 s | Quick montage: ten planes, ten bonks, planes piling on the desk. The creator looks at the lens, deadpan. | Rapid bonk-bonk-bonk, a rising laugh track-free beat. | "Done pitching?" |
| 5-10 s | The creator sweeps all planes into a bin, closes the laptop with a thunk, picks up the camera, grins. A presenter (second actor) strolls in with a clipboard, leans in, glances at the camera. | Thunk, camera click, upbeat bass. Presenter: "Apply once. Someone looks at your channel." | "Apply once." / "A person looks at your channel." |
| 10-15 s | Creator films happily while the presenter hovers nearby, giving a thumbs up that is only about the application. | Bass drop, final ding. | "A sponsor chosen for your small channel." |
| End card | Standard. | Sting. | Standard. |

- **Why a small YouTuber stops:** slapstick and a recognisable cringe (cold emailing) they will laugh at.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the cringe goes away. The ad says "no cold emails" and "apply once". It does not claim a result.
- **Not stock, not a rival's:** the boomerang planes are a visual gag that is only about this offer.
- **AI difficulty:** medium. **Main risk:** the paper plane loop physics. Mitigation: cut on the bonk, hide the return flight with a whoosh and a smash cut.
- **Static version:** a creator with a paper plane stuck to their forehead, "No cold emails. Free to apply.", Apply button.

## C3. Two Marbles

**One-line pitch:** A marble run shows the terms: two marbles, two paths, and the "no deal" path ends in a soft landing.

- **Angle:** 3, Free to Try.
- **ONE promise:** Free to apply. No deal, no commission.

**Outline**

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Macro on a candy-coloured marble run. Two marbles (coral, blue) drop at once. | Marble click-clack, a tight hi-hat. | "Free to apply. No deal, no commission." |
| 2-5 s | Both marbles roll through a bright entry gate that is wide open. The gate has a blank round sign. | Click-clack, a rising synth. | "Free to apply." |
| 5-10 s | The path splits. The coral marble drops onto a soft cushion with a squishy thud. The blue marble rolls through a small toll arm that lowers and lifts again. | Squish, then a clean "ting". | "No deal? No commission." / "Deal done? Commission comes out of your fee." |
| 10-15 s | Both marbles settle. A hand taps the cushion marble and it bounces, happy. | Bounce, drum fill. | "Nothing upfront." |
| End card | Standard. | Sting. | Standard. |

- **Why a small YouTuber stops:** marble runs are deeply satisfying and the sound is addictive. The text is simple.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the fear "it will cost me" is answered by a picture. No money is ever shown.
- **Not stock, not a rival's:** no other sponsor offer explains terms with a marble run.
- **AI difficulty:** hard. **Main risk:** marble physics and the split path. Mitigation: shoot the two paths as separate clips, cut together, and add the toll arm graphic.
- **Static version:** two marbles on a split track, one on a cushion, "Free to apply. No deal, no commission.", Apply button.

## C4. Big Red Button

**One-line pitch:** You sit at a control desk. Sponsor boxes roll by. Nothing moves until you press yes.

- **Angle:** 4, Your Yes.
- **ONE promise:** You can turn down any sponsor. Nothing goes ahead without your yes.

**Outline**

| Time | We see | We hear | Words on screen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | First-person hands over a control desk. A giant red button, a giant green button. Pastel gift boxes sit on a still conveyor. | Arcade power-up chime. | "Turn down any sponsor. Free to apply." |
| 2-5 s | The belt starts. A box rolls close. A thumb slams the red button. The box is flung off with a boing. | Boing, cartoon whoosh. | "You can say no." |
| 5-10 s | The next box rolls up. The belt stops by itself. The finger hovers over the green button. Nothing moves. | A long held note, a heartbeat tick. | "Nothing goes ahead without your yes." |
| 10-15 s | The thumb presses green. Soft "thup". The box nudges forward one centimetre. | Satisfying click, brass sting. | "Your channel. Your yes." |
| End card | Standard. | Sting. | Standard. |

- **Why a small YouTuber stops:** the loud red button and the boing are pure sensory fun. The hands are POV so it feels like them.
- **Why they tap Apply:** control is the thing they fear to lose. The ad shows they keep it.
- **Not stock, not a rival's:** the big-button desk and the flicked boxes make a distinct, repeatable image.
- **AI difficulty:** easy. **Main risk:** the belt stopping on cue. Mitigation: shoot the stop as its own clip.
- **Static version:** a thumb over a red and a green button, "You can say no to any sponsor.", "Free to apply.", Apply button.

# Compliance test (section 9 and section 10)

**Section 10, bland test: every concept passes.**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| 1. Offer on screen in the first 2 s | Pass. Every hook and every C1 to C8 first row carries "Free to apply" or "no deal, no commission". |
| 2. Understood within 5 s | Pass. The qualifier chip appears by 3 s and the benefit by 5 s. |
| 3. One specific promise | Pass. Each concept names one promise (size, no pitching, free, your yes, a person, or the terms). |
| 4. Not stock or a rival's | Pass. Each concept has its own physical set piece (height board, boomerang planes, marble run, big button, miniature, jingle, split desks, claw machine). |
| 5. Never about the company | Pass. No story, mission or founder. Only what the viewer gets. |

**Section 9, hard rules: every concept passes.**

- No numbers of creators, sponsors or deals. No money amounts, no percentages, no commission rates, no "2 working days". Only the confirmed "1,000 to 50,000" and a graphic "0" (permitted in section 8).
- No "you will get a sponsor", "guaranteed", "get sponsored" or equivalent. The promise is always "a person picks a sponsor to suggest" and the end card says "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor."
- No AI person poses as a customer, reviews the offer or describes a result. Presenters only talk to the viewer about the offer.
- No countdowns, deadlines or "last places".
- No testimonials, ratings, "creators love it" or named brands. All gift boxes and props are blank and unbranded.
- No real logos (not YouTube's), no real people, no copyrighted music or characters. C6's jingle must be original.
- All words, numbers, ticks and the logo are added graphics. Nothing readable is generated in the scene.

**Fixes made during the test**

- Removed an early vending-machine idea ("press the button, a sponsor drops out"). It read as "you will get a sponsor".
- Removed a game-show confetti finish. Confetti reads as a win, which implies a result. All end beats are "ding" or "thunk", not celebration of an outcome.
- C3: replaced coins at the toll arm with a plain arm and a "ting". No money is shown.
- C7: the calm-desk creator is shown only filming after one tap. They never say they got a sponsor.
- C8: the gift box stays closed. It never opens to reveal a result.
- H6: "Tailored" is kept because the brief says a person chooses by hand. It does not claim a result.
- H9: "Pay only on a deal" kept as a plain statement of the terms. No amounts.
