# Poster concepts, batch 2

Poster Copywriter and Concept Lead. 24 November 2026. American spelling throughout.

Fixed on every poster (not repeated below): footer strip with the real logo, Apply, smallfold.netlify.app/apply, "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor.", and "Image made with AI" on any poster that uses a generated picture. The blank is always the thick pen-blue bar and is never filled. The logo never sits next to the blank.

Support line used unless stated: **"Got 1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free."** Tight version (T): **"1,000 to 50,000 subs? Free to apply for a sponsor."**

Note on the support line: it is a question in the brief's own wording. It is a statement about eligibility, not a yes/no hook, so I keep it. If we want zero question marks on the wall, use T with a full stop: "1,000 to 50,000 subs. Free to apply for a sponsor."

## PART 1. Twelve concepts

### P1. The Monument (seed 1, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  THIS VIDEO
  IS SPONSORED
  BY ____
  (6 words plus the blank)
- **Alternatives:** (a) SPONSORED BY ____ (the whole poster is these three words and the bar) (b) THIS CHANNEL IS SPONSORED BY ____
- **Support:** T, set tiny, bottom left.
- **Visual:** The sentence is enormous, cream on ink, slab-edge to slab-edge in Anton, so the type is the architecture. The only small things are the support line and footer. Eye order: the blue bar (the only color), then "SPONSORED", then the small line that says what to do.
- **Type or image:** Type-led. No picture.
- **Where the blank is:** Last line, a bar as tall as the capitals, wider than the words before it.
- **One-second test:** "Something about a sponsor and a missing name."
- **Honesty check:** The unfilled bar could read as "a sponsor is coming". Safe because it is empty, nothing is promised, and the footer says applying doesn't promise one. No result, no dig.
- **Wall test:** 9/10. The oldest sentence on YouTube, made huge. You stop because the gap looks wrong.

### P2. The One-Person Film (seed 2, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  ALL BY YOU.
  SPONSORED BY
  ____
  (5 words plus the blank)
- **Alternatives:** (a) FILMED BY YOU. SPONSORED BY ____ (b) YOUR CHANNEL. SPONSORED BY ____
- **Support:** T, set as the last line of the billing block.
- **Visual:** A cinema one-sheet, black field, with ALL BY YOU. as the giant title. Under it a tall condensed billing block in small caps: "Written by you / Filmed by you / Edited by you / Posted by you", all tiny, as texture, like real credits. The enormous thing is the title, the small thing is the credit block, and the eye goes title, blue bar, then reads down the credits to the point.
- **Type or image:** Type-led. No picture.
- **Where the blank is:** Last line of the title, then repeated as the final credit "Sponsored by ____" in the block.
- **One-second test:** "A movie poster for a one-person channel that has no sponsor."
- **Honesty check:** The seed's 12-word headline broke the 7-word rule, so the credits moved to texture and the headline is 5 words. "All by you" is a statement about the creator's workload, not a result. No dig. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 8/10. Film-poster grammar is recognized from across the street. Loses a point for the billing block needing a second look.

### P3. The Checklist (seed 3, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  FIRST SPONSOR
  ☐
  (2 words plus an empty box)
- **Alternatives:** (a) NEXT ON THE LIST: SPONSOR (b) TICK, TICK, TICK. THEN A SPONSOR ____
- **Support:** The brief's line, under the list.
- **Visual:** Big hand-ticked list in marker on cream: "First video ✓ / First comment ✓ / First 1,000 subs ✓" in medium size, and then the last item, "First sponsor", set 3x bigger with an empty box drawn in pen blue. The ticks are the busy part, the empty box is the loud part. Eye: the empty box, then up the ticks, then "sponsor".
- **Type or image:** Type-led (hand-drawn ticks as simple shapes). No picture.
- **Where the blank is:** The box, drawn thick, in pen blue.
- **One-second test:** "A to-do list with one thing not done: a sponsor."
- **Honesty check:** An empty box that follows ticked ones can imply "it comes next, automatically". Fixed by the headline wording "FIRST SPONSOR" with no verb and by the support line saying "Apply". "First 1,000 subs" is the eligibility fact, not a result we claim for anyone. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 8/10. Everyone knows the feeling of a box not ticked.

### P4. Not 100K (seed 4, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  APPLY FOR
  A SPONSOR
  AT 1K
  (6 words)
- **Alternatives:** (a) 1K SUBS. APPLY FOR A SPONSOR. (b) NOT 100K. 1K. SPONSOR.
- **Support:** T.
- **Visual:** A giant "100K" struck through with one rough stroke of black marker, set on a loud yellow field, so large it bleeds off the edge. Small beside it, clean, "1K" sits like a fact. Eye: the strike, then "1K", then the line.
- **Type or image:** Type-led. No picture.
- **Where the blank is:** Under the headline, a short bar and the words "Sponsored by" small.
- **One-second test:** "You don't need a huge channel to ask for a sponsor."
- **Honesty check:** Real risk: a channel with 80,000 subs may think the poster is for them, and the strike could read as a dig at big channels. Fix: the support line must carry "1,000 to 50,000", and the strike is of a number, never of a type of creator. Also "1K" means 1,000 and must never be rounded for anyone under 1,000. Usable, but the least safe of the batch.
- **Wall test:** 7/10. Strong gesture, but needs a beat of reading.

### P5. The Name Tag (seed 5, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  HELLO,
  MY SPONSOR
  IS ____
  (4 words plus the blank)
- **Alternatives:** (a) HELLO, SPONSOR. (b) MY SPONSOR IS ____
- **Support:** T.
- **Visual:** A single huge name-tag badge fills most of the poster: a rounded plain rectangle with a heavy band across the top, flat color, no brand marks. The badge is enormous, the support line and footer are tiny on the plain wall behind it. Eye: the blue bar inside the badge, then HELLO, then the line.
- **Type or image:** Type-led, built from a rounded rectangle. Could be a photographed sticker, but pure shapes do it better.
- **Where the blank is:** The handwriting field of the badge. The one place a blank is expected, which is why it works.
- **One-second test:** "A name tag, and the name is missing."
- **Honesty check:** "My sponsor is ____" could read as "you will have one". Safe because the blank is never filled and the support line says "Apply". Avoid anything like "meet your sponsor". Note: the red-banner "Hello my name is" sticker is a common format; do not copy a branded version, draw our own.
- **Wall test:** 7/10. Friendly and instant, but a familiar trick.

### P6. The Packshot (seed 6, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  TODAY'S SPONSOR:
  ____
  (2 words plus the blank)
- **Alternatives:** (a) SPONSOR OF THE DAY: ____ (b) FEATURED SPONSOR: ____
- **Support:** T.
- **Visual:** A plain white box shot like a luxury product on a flat saturated color, hard side light, a long shadow. The box is the hero and is blank on its face. The headline is small and set like a boutique label. Eye: the box, the long shadow, the label.
- **Type or image:** Image-led. ONE picture: a plain matte white box with no markings, centered on a flat single-color seamless, hard light from the left, no text, no faces. Could be done as pure shapes: a CSS box with three flat faces and a gradient shadow. The photo only adds realism, so this is the one to drop if pictures run short.
- **Where the blank is:** After the label. The pen-blue bar sits on the box face.
- **One-second test:** "A product, but no brand on it. Sponsor goes here."
- **Honesty check:** "Today's sponsor" could suggest a sponsor exists today. Fixed by changing to "SPONSOR OF THE DAY" only if a reviewer is strict; otherwise it is safe because there is no name and no promise. "Featured" would suggest a result, so it should not be used.
- **Wall test:** 7/10. Beautiful, but the joke is quieter than P1 or P9.

### P7. The Wanted Notice (seed 7, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  WANTED:
  A SPONSOR
  (3 words)
- **Alternatives:** (a) WANTED: A SPONSOR FOR A SMALL CHANNEL (b) SPONSOR WANTED. APPLY WITHIN.
- **Support:** The brief's line, set like the small print at the bottom of an old notice.
- **Visual:** Cream paper, a giant WANTED in condensed slab, and a big empty frame where the mug shot would be. The frame is the hero and it is empty. Under it "Name: ____" in typewriter. Eye: WANTED, the empty frame, the blue name line.
- **Type or image:** Type-led. No picture (empty frame drawn as a shape). No faces, no person, ever.
- **Where the blank is:** The "Name" line under the empty frame.
- **One-second test:** "A wanted poster for a sponsor, not a person."
- **Honesty check:** No "reward" (that would be money). "Wanted" reads as the channel wanting a sponsor, not a promise one will be found. It must not be read as the company hunting. "Apply within" is fine since that is the one action. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 7.5/10. A format everyone reads instantly.

### P8. The Empty Plinth (seed 8, sharpened)
- **Headline, as set:**
  ROOM FOR
  A SPONSOR
  (4 words)
- **Alternatives:** (a) SPONSOR GOES HERE (b) ON DISPLAY: ____
- **Support:** T, set like a museum label on the plinth.
- **Visual:** A tall white plinth lit by a hard spotlight against a deep teal wall. The plinth is enormous and dead center, there is nothing on top of it, and the lit empty space is the whole idea. A small museum label sits on the plinth side. Eye: the light, the empty top, the label.
- **Type or image:** Image-led. ONE picture: an empty white museum plinth on a flat dark seamless, hard single spotlight from above-left, strong shadow, no objects, no text, no people. Could be done as shapes (a rectangle, a gradient cone), but a photograph gives the drama that makes it a poster.
- **Where the blank is:** The museum label: "Sponsor: ____".
- **One-second test:** "An empty pedestal in a gallery. Waiting for a sponsor."
- **Honesty check:** "Waiting" and "goes here" push toward a guarantee. Fixed by using "ROOM FOR", which only says a space exists. "SPONSOR GOES HERE" as an alternative would be riskier. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 7.5/10. Elegant and quiet. Stops people who like design, not everyone.

### P9. Sponsor Not Included (own)
- **Headline, as set:**
  SPONSOR
  NOT
  INCLUDED
  (3 words)
- **Alternatives:** (a) SPONSOR: NOT INCLUDED (b) SPONSOR SOLD SEPARATELY (rejected for "sold", see Part 4 note)
- **Support:** The brief's line.
- **Visual:** A flat packaging-orange field. "SPONSOR" is huge, "NOT INCLUDED" is huge too, stacked like a warning label. In tiny spec-sheet type in a corner: "Includes: ideas, camera, editing, late nights." Eye: the giant words, the blue bar, the tiny list.
- **Type or image:** Type-led. No picture.
- **Where the blank is:** A bar under the last word, the width of the block, like the empty line of a spec sheet.
- **One-second test:** "Everything comes in the box except a sponsor."
- **Honesty check:** It plays "batteries not included" and says nothing about money or results. The dig risk is that "not included" could read as a flaw in the creator. It does not: it names what the one-person channel cannot give itself. The small list is general, not a claim about anyone. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 9/10. A phrase everyone knows, one word swapped. The best single line here.

### P10. Dear Sponsor (own)
- **Headline, as set:**
  DEAR
  SPONSOR,
  (2 words)
- **Alternatives:** (a) TO: SPONSOR. FROM: A SMALL CHANNEL. (b) DEAR ____,
- **Support:** The brief's line, set as the body of the letter, three lines, handwritten look.
- **Visual:** A plain ruled letter on a bright desk-blue field... pen blue stays for the blank only, so the field is warm red. DEAR SPONSOR is huge. The body is tiny, like a real letter's first lines. Eye: DEAR SPONSOR, the blue line under it, the small letter.
- **Type or image:** Type-led. No picture.
- **Where the blank is:** The signature line at the foot: "Yours, ____".
- **One-second test:** "A letter written to a sponsor."
- **Honesty check:** "Dear sponsor" speaks to nobody in particular. No result, no promise, no dig. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 6.5/10. Warm, but the gap is less visible than the others.

### P11. The Height Chart (own)
- **Headline, as set:**
  THIS TALL TO
  APPLY FOR
  A SPONSOR
  (7 words)
- **Alternatives:** (a) 1,000 SUBS IS TALL ENOUGH. APPLY FOR A SPONSOR. (b) HEIGHT: 1,000 TO 50,000 SUBS
- **Support:** T.
- **Visual:** A tall fairground height-limit post, running the full height of the poster, in loud yellow with black stripes. The post is enormous and the two notches ("1,000" and "50,000") are small. The headline sits beside the lower notch. Eye: the post, the lower notch, the headline.
- **Type or image:** Pure shapes and type. No picture.
- **Where the blank is:** At the top of the post, where a sign would hang: "Sponsored by ____".
- **One-second test:** "A ride sign saying you have to be a certain size."
- **Honesty check:** The two notches are the real eligibility range, so it is accurate. Risk: someone under 1,000 could feel excluded, and the upper notch could feel like a dig at big channels. Fix: the notches are set neutrally, with no "too small" or "too big" wording. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 8/10. The ride sign is understood in a flash, and the shape is simple to draw.

### P12. The Marker (own)
- **Headline, as set:**
  SPONSORS,
  PICKED
  BY HAND.
  (4 words)
- **Alternatives:** (a) A PERSON PICKS SPONSORS. NO ALGORITHM. (b) NO ALGORITHM. SPONSORS BY HAND.
- **Support:** The brief's line.
- **Visual:** One huge fat marker lying diagonally across the poster, cap off, drawing a thick pen-blue stroke across the field that becomes the blank bar. The marker is enormous. The headline is small, beside the stroke. Eye: marker tip, stroke, headline.
- **Type or image:** Image-led. ONE picture: a single fat marker, cut out on a transparent background, hard side light, a long soft shadow, no text on the barrel, no hand, no faces. Could be done as simple shapes (a rounded rectangle and a cone), which would look flat but would still work as a graphic poster.
- **Where the blank is:** The marker's stroke itself. It ends where the bar ends.
- **One-second test:** "Someone is drawing the blank by hand."
- **Honesty check:** "Picked by hand" is the exact fact ("By hand. No automatic list, no algorithm"). Risk: "picked" could read as "a sponsor has been picked for you". Fixed by the plural "SPONSORS" and the support line "Apply". The alternative with "NO ALGORITHM" must not name any other company or platform's algorithm. Safe.
- **Wall test:** 7/10. A strong object, but the idea is a notch quieter.

## PART 4. Three lines I was tempted to write and rejected

1. **"Your sponsor is waiting."** Reads as a guarantee ("you will get a sponsor") and as pressure. A sponsor is only suggested after a person looks at the channel, and only if you say yes.
2. **"Big channels get sponsors. Why not you?"** A dig at big channels, a question that can be answered "no", and a hint that you will get one. It also breaks the 50,000 ceiling on who can apply.
3. **"Reserved for: ____"** (and "Sponsor sold separately"). "Reserved" promises a place that nobody has promised, and "sold" brings money into the line when we must never hint at price. This is why P9's alternative (b) is struck.
