# UGC ideas for the Smallfold creator offer (v2)

Sources: `product-brief.md` (section 2 facts, section 9 rules, section 10 bland test), `angles.md`, `research.md`. Nothing from v1.

The offer: **A sponsor chosen for your small YouTube channel. Free to apply. You pay only if a deal is done.** For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers. One call to action: **Apply**.

## Rules that apply to all six

- **No customer voice.** Nobody on camera says they applied, got a sponsor, or saw any result. Presenters talk TO the viewer. Skits act out the situation before applying and are labelled as acted.
- **Label on every video:** "AI presenter" (presenter concepts) or "Made with AI · Acted scene" (U2), in a corner for the full length, added as a graphic.
- **All text and numbers are graphics added in the edit.** Generated shots show no readable screens, no lettering, no logos. Laptops face away from camera; the phone on a tripod only shows its back or a blurred screen.
- **Pace.** Speech stays at or under 2.3 words per second per clip (most clips sit at 1.7 to 2.2). Numbers are counted as spoken ("one thousand to fifty thousand" is 5 words). Clips are 4 to 15 seconds, each generated whole; the edit cuts inside a clip to keep it quick.
- **Sound.** Voice first. A light, upbeat, licensed instrumental bed under it, low. Small sound effects. Captions mirror the voice. No copyrighted music.
- **Look.** Bright daylight, real-looking lived-in creator spaces. No dark or moody rooms.
- **End card (all six).** Fixed elements, placed from real files: the big "1,000 to 50,000" with "YouTube subscribers", the "Apply" button, smallfold.netlify.app/apply, the real logo, and the small line "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." Each concept below names its "Apply" line and its one supporting line. Nothing else on the card. Keep key text inside the Reels/Shorts safe zone (research.md section 4, second-hand figures; verify before export).

## U1. Five Fingers

**Format:** 1. Demo of the application (described, not shown on a screen).
**Pitch:** A presenter counts the five things on one hand to show how short the application is, then says what happens next and that you can say no.

**On camera:** Woman in her late 20s, curly dark hair, mustard cardigan. Where: a sunny kitchen-counter desk, window light, a plant, a phone on a tripod, a mug, a few sticky notes. Lived in, not staged.

**Spoken script (word for word)**

| Clip | Seconds | Line | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0 to 6 | "Sponsor offer: free to apply. One thousand to fifty thousand subs? That's you." | 13 |
| B | 6 to 12 | "It's five things. Name. Email. Channel link. What it's about. Sub count." | 12 |
| C | 12 to 19 | "A person looks at your channel, picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no." | 15 |
| D | 19 to 23 | "No deal, no commission. Apply." | 5 |

**Total: 45 words, 23 seconds (about 2.0 words per second).** The offer ("sponsor offer: free to apply") is spoken in the first 2 seconds.

**Shot list**

| Shot | Seconds | What we see | On-screen text (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (clip A) | 0.0 to 2.0 | Tight handheld selfie angle, she points at the lens, window glow behind her. | Huge "1,000 to 50,000", under it "YouTube subscribers". Lower third: "A sponsor chosen for your small channel. Free to apply." Corner: "AI presenter" |
| 2 (clip A) | 2.0 to 6.0 | Cut-in: she holds her hands wide like a measuring range, then taps the air at "you". | The number pulses once. |
| 3 (clip B) | 6.0 to 12.0 | One hand, she counts a finger per beat. Quick cut-ins to the tripod phone and the mug between beats. | Pop-ups, one per beat: "1 Name", "2 Email", "3 Channel link", "4 What it's about", "5 Sub count" |
| 4 (clip C) | 12.0 to 15.5 | Medium shot, she points at the tripod phone's lens. | "A person looks at your channel" with an eye icon |
| 5 (clip C) | 15.5 to 19.0 | Close-up, her hand waves something away to the side, then a tick. | "You can say no." |
| 6 (clip D) | 19.0 to 23.0 | She smiles and taps down toward the lens. End card builds. | End card |

**3 alternative first lines**
1. "One thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply free."
2. "Small channel? This sponsor offer is for you."
3. "The sponsor application is five things. Count with me."

**End card:** Button "Apply". Supporting line: "Free to apply. No deal, no commission."

**Why it won't look like every other UGC ad:** It is a demo that never shows a screen. The five things become five fingers and five pop-ups on a beat, so "short application" is felt in 6 seconds without explaining a process. The giant subscriber range is the first frame, not a logo.

## U2. Sent.

**Format:** 2. POV skit.
**Pitch:** A creator-at-a-desk bit: a hopeful cold email, a long silent refresh, then a deadpan turn to camera with the offer. Clearly acted, labelled as such.

**On camera:** Man in his mid-30s, short dark hair, green shirt with rolled sleeves. Where: a bright spare-room desk with a ring light, a spider plant by the window, a wall of sticky notes, a laptop with its back to the camera.

**Spoken script (word for word)**

| Clip | Seconds | Line | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0 to 4 | "Skip pitching. Free sponsor application. Quick scene." | 7 |
| B | 4 to 11 | (He reads aloud as he types, in a hopeful voice.) "Dear Brand. Love your stuff. Sponsor me? Thanks. Sent." | 9 |
| C | 11 to 19 | (Deadpan, to camera.) "No cold emails. Apply once. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no." | 16 |
| D | 19 to 23 | "No deal, no commission. Apply." | 5 |

**Total: 37 words, 23 seconds (about 1.6 words per second).** Clip B has 7 seconds for 9 words, so there is room for typing sounds and a silent beat. The offer is spoken in the first 2 seconds.

**Shot list**

| Shot | Seconds | What we see | On-screen text (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (clip A) | 0.0 to 4.0 | He is mid-sentence at the desk, one finger up at the lens, laptop open behind him, back to camera. | Big "A sponsor chosen for your small channel. Free to apply." Corner: "Made with AI · Acted scene" |
| 2 (clip B) | 4.0 to 7.0 | Hands pounding keys, a flourish on the last key. Cut to his face, proud. | None |
| 3 (clip B) | 7.0 to 11.0 | He stares at the laptop's back. Long silence. A leaf drops off the spider plant. He tilts his head. | Small "Acted scene" tag; no other text |
| 4 (clip C) | 11.0 to 15.0 | He swivels slowly to camera, closes the laptop with one finger. | "No cold emails." then "Apply once." |
| 5 (clip C) | 15.0 to 19.0 | Cut-in: his thumb pushes something off-frame, then a tick. | "A person picks a sponsor to suggest." then "You can say no." |
| 6 (clip D) | 19.0 to 23.0 | He gives a small nod to camera. End card builds. | End card |

**3 alternative first lines**
1. "Stop pitching sponsors. Apply once instead."
2. "Cold emails cringe? Free sponsor application."
3. "No more cold emails. Free to apply."

**End card:** Button "Apply". Supporting line: "No cold emails. Free to apply."

**Why it won't look like every other UGC ad:** It is a short sketch with a silent punchline, not a person praising a product. The comedy is the quiet refresh, and the turn to camera is the pitch. The viewer's own cringe does the selling.

**On camera:** Man in his late 50s, grey beard, denim apron over a tee. Where: a bright garage workshop with the big door open to daylight, pegboard of tools, camera on a tripod, a wooden door frame covered in pencil height marks.

**Spoken script (word for word)**

| Clip | Seconds | Line | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0 to 5 | "Too small? Free sponsor application. Stand here a second." | 9 |
| B | 5 to 11 | "This offer's built for channels with one thousand to fifty thousand subs." | 12 |
| C | 11 to 17 | "A person picks a sponsor for your channel. You can say no." | 12 |
| D | 17 to 21 | "No deal, no commission. Apply." | 5 |

**Total: 38 words, 21 seconds (about 1.8 words per second).** The offer lands inside the first 2 seconds.

**Shot list**

| Shot | Seconds | What we see | On-screen text (added later) |

## U6. Step Right

**Format:** 6. Free choice: a yes/no "can you apply?" quiz, hosted by a presenter who points the way and never answers for herself.

**Why this format suits the offer better than unboxing or ASMR:** There is no product to unbox and nothing to hear or touch. What this offer does have is a gate (YouTube only, 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, a person picks, you can say no). A quiz makes the viewer check themselves in 12 seconds, so the right people self-select and the wrong ones scroll on. Each question is one fact from the brief, and the "yes" step acts out "nothing goes ahead without your yes". Unboxing and ASMR would invent a thing to show.

**Pitch:** Three yes/no questions, with a taped floor line for the viewer to "step right" on. The presenter points; the viewer answers.

**On camera:** Woman in her mid-60s, silver bob, coral blazer, round earrings. Where: a bright studio corner with a rolled white paper backdrop, a strip of tape across the floor, a softbox, a plant.

**Spoken script (word for word)**

| Clip | Seconds | Line | Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 0 to 4 | "Sponsor offer, free to apply. Three questions." | 7 |
| B | 4 to 8 | "Got a YouTube channel? Step right for yes." | 8 |
| C | 8 to 13 | "One thousand to fifty thousand subs? Step right again." | 9 |
| D | 13 to 19 | "Fine with a person choosing the sponsor? You can say no." | 11 |
| E | 19 to 23 | "Three yeses? Apply. No deal, no commission." | 7 |

**Total: 42 words, 23 seconds (about 1.8 words per second).** The offer lands inside the first 2 seconds.

**Shot list**

| Shot | Seconds | What we see | On-screen text (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (clip A) | 0.0 to 4.0 | Wide shot of the taped line on the floor, she stands to the side holding up three fingers. | "A sponsor chosen for your small channel. Free to apply." Corner: "AI presenter" |
| 2 (clip B) | 4.0 to 8.0 | She sweeps her arm to the right of the line. | "1 YouTube channel?" "NO" on the left floor, "YES" with a tick on the right |
| 3 (clip C) | 8.0 to 13.0 | She holds out both hands, like a range, then sweeps right again. | "2" and "1,000 to 50,000 subscribers?" "YES" tick lights up again |
| 4 (clip D) | 13.0 to 19.0 | Close-up, she raises an eyebrow, then waves a hand "away", then a thumb up. | "3 A person chooses a sponsor to suggest", "You can say no to any" |
| 5 (clip E) | 19.0 to 23.0 | She holds up three fingers, then taps down toward the lens. End card builds. | End card |

**3 alternative first lines**
1. "Step right if you run a small channel."
2. "Three yes-or-no questions. Free to apply."
3. "YouTube channel? Step right. Sponsor offer, free."

**End card:** Button "Apply". Supporting line: "For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers."

**Why it won't look like every other UGC ad:** The viewer does the acting, and the presenter never claims to be one of them. The taped floor line and YES/NO graphics turn the offer's rules into a game. A host in her 60s with a silver bob breaks the usual creator-ad casting.

## Claims audit (done before finishing)

I read every spoken line again against: personal experience with the service, a result, money, a guarantee, urgency. All 6 scripts pass: no "I applied", "it worked", "I got", no money or rates, no "guaranteed", "will get", "limited", "now" or "today".

Drafts I cut for this reason:
- U3: "It's not too small" (reads as a promise of fit). It now says the offer is "built for" the range.
- U1: "Then a person reads it" (not a fact in section 2). It now says "looks at your channel".
- U2: a draft pitch email with "two thousand subscribers" (a number). The line is now generic.
- U1 and U5: "That's it" after the five things (implies nothing else follows). Removed.
- U6: the presenter stepping right herself (she would be posing as a creator who qualifies). She now points and the viewer answers.

Fact check: every spoken claim maps to product-brief section 2 (who it is for, a person looks and chooses, you can say no, joining is free, no deal no commission, five things in the application). The commission rates and the "2 working days" line are not used.
