# 1. The Line (score 41)

Vertical 9:16 for YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. Style: see `style.md`. All words on screen are graphics added in the edit; none is generated.

## Pitch and promise

- **Pitch:** A small creator rehearses "This video is sponsored by" again and again, cut off before the name every time. Then they tell you that you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Voice:** On-camera speech (creator, lip-synced or dubbed); no narrator.
- **Total length:** 25.6 s (22.6 s of story plus a 3.0 s end card).
- **Shots:** 10 rows in the table (some rows are two beats of one generated clip, split where the caption changes). Hard cuts only.
- **Generated clips:** 4, 5 + 6 + 9 + 9 s each, 29 s of footage in all (22.6 s used on screen).
- **Signature sound:** The pop of the cut-off line. A dry, bubble-like pop on every "by", then a light snap-and-clap beat (about 100 bpm) that builds over the four takes and drops out the moment the creator turns to the viewer.

Blank: "____" is always drawn as a thick pen-blue underline (`#2B44D6`) and never filled.

## What a muted viewer reads in the first 3 seconds

Tags "AI actor" and "Pretend", the counter "Take 1", the chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." (from 0.0), the caption "This video is sponsored by ____" (from 0.3), and the chip "1,000 to 50,000 subs" (from 2.0). By second 3 a muted viewer has read the sponsor offer, that it is free, the subscriber range, and the unfinished line.

## Two alternative openings for hook tests

1. **Blank first.** Open 0.0 to 2.0 on giant type only: the headline "This video is sponsored by ____" on a yellow plate over a blurred still of the grin, one pop at 1.8, then hard cut into Take 1. All words of the script stay and keep their order; the whole ad moves 2 s later.
2. **Take 3 first.** Open on the whisper close-up with the counter "Take 3" and the caption "Sponsored by ____", pop at 1.0, then jump to Take 1 and run on. Only the order of the first two spoken fragments changes, so this needs a fresh approval of the script order.

All captions, labels and the blank stay the same. Changes to the order of spoken words need the owner's approval.

## Risks for AI generation and how the shots avoid them

| Risk | How the design avoids it |
|---|---|
| Same person across 4 clips | Build one character reference still (hoodie colour, hair, room). Start every clip from that still or the last frame of the clip before. The cut-off device and the hard punch-ins hide small drifts. |
| Words in the scene | The prompt forbids posters, writing on the wall, a logo on the hoodie, or a readable phone or tripod screen. The phone on the tripod is shown from behind. Every caption, chip, counter and tag is a graphic added afterwards. |
| Hands | Take 4 uses open palms, not pointing and not counting. The nods in the talking section carry the "facts" instead of fingers. |
| Four retakes of one line | Three different deliveries in one clip may blur together; the fallback is three 4 s clips from the same still. The cut always lands on a closed mouth, so lip-sync never has to finish the word. |
| Speech pace | 41 words, no sentence faster than 2.3 words per second (table below). The retakes are slow and short on purpose. |
| Honesty | The line is never finished, in any take. "Pretend" is on screen 0.5 s before the first spoken word and stays on every rehearsal take. |

## Honesty check

- No frame shows or names a sponsor, a brand, money, a result or a celebration.
- The blank is empty in every frame including the end card.
- "AI actor" is on every row with a person. "Pretend" is on every acted sponsor moment, up before the first spoken word of it.
- The end card carries "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor."
