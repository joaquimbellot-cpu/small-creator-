# The leader's selection (version 2)

**Date:** 24 November 2026. This is the final choice of concepts to sketch. Scores are the Conversion Critic's, from `scorecard.md` (round 1) and its round 2 section. The fixes the Critic asked for are already applied below.

## How the choice was made

- 30 concepts were written in two rounds and scored out of 50. The bar was 40, plus the bland test.
- Round 1: 1 of 16 survived. Almost every idea answered an objection ("too small", "costs money") and none sold the wanting.
- Round 2 was written on the desire itself: being a sponsored creator. 2 of 14 survived, and one more is one fix from the bar.
- Kills that stay dead: every metaphor concept (dolls, marbles, claw machine, football kit), every "presenter reads the facts" concept, the announcer and trailer voices, and anything where a fast viewer could think the person already has a sponsor.

## The campaign idea that ties them together: the blank

Every surviving concept is the same sentence with the same hole in it: **"Sponsored by ____."**

A small YouTuber already does every job on the channel. The one thing they cannot give themselves is the sponsor. So each ad shows the sponsor-shaped hole in a place where they will recognise it (the line they rehearse, the product they would hold, the credits, the segment in the video, the milestone shelf) and then says the hole is something they can apply to fill: free, from 1,000 subscribers.

The blank is honest by design. It never shows a sponsor, a brand or a result. It stays empty in every frame, including the end card.

## 1. The Line (41)

**Pitch:** A small creator rehearses "This video is sponsored by" again and again, cut off before the name every time. Then they tell you that you can apply for a sponsor, free.

**Promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.

**On camera:** invented creator in their early 20s, bright bedroom studio in daylight, ring light, camera on a tripod, coloured wall.

**Spoken script (41 words, about 18 s):** "This video is sponsored by. Sponsored by. Sponsored by. Sponsored by. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? You can apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

**Must not be lost:** the line is never finished. No name, no brand, no "got it" moment, in any take.

**Fixes applied:** "picks one for your channel" is now "picks a sponsor to suggest". The line is burned in as a caption ("This video is sponsored by ____") so it works muted. The "1,000 to 50,000 subs" chip arrives at second 2. A "Pretend" tag sits on the rehearsal takes.

## 2. Missing Sponsor (40)

**Pitch:** A cook does a full, sincere sponsor read for a plain white box with nothing on it, turns it over looking for a name, and finds none.

**Promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.

**On camera:** invented cooking creator in their 30s, bright kitchen, daylight, phone on a tripod. Prop: one plain matte white box, no text, no marks.

**Spoken script (42 words, about 18 s):** "Huge thanks to today's sponsor. Fits right in the kitchen. Comes in three colours. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

**Must not be lost:** the stamp "Missing: the sponsor" is on screen from the first frame, before the voice speaks. The read is nonsense aimed at a blank box. The beat is the look for a name that is not there.

**Fixes applied:** stamp and "Pretend" tag come before the thanks line. "You can say no to any of them" is now "You can say no." The subscriber range moves up to seconds 4 to 6. The invisible product is now a blank white box, because AI video handles a held box better than empty presenting hands.

## 3. The Credits (40)

**Pitch:** The end credits of a one-person channel: "Filmed by: you. Edited by: you. Sponsored by: ____".

**Promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.

**On camera:** invented creator in their late 20s on a bright home set, doing every job in quick cuts. The credits are large type over the picture.

**Spoken script (39 words, about 17 s):** "Filmed by you. Edited by you. Sponsored by. One line's missing. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

**Must not be lost:** the last credit line stays blank in every frame, including the end card. The credits are type and must read with the sound off.

**Fixes applied:** cut to three credit lines so "Sponsored by: ____" lands by second 3 to 4. "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." is large in the first 2 seconds. The cold-tea pour is now a mug sitting on the desk. No screen is ever in shot.

## 4. The Gap (40 after revision)

**Pitch:** A cook's video reaches "the sponsor segment" and nothing happens: he holds a TV-host pose at a blank wall while crickets play.

**Promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.

**On camera:** invented cook in his 30s, beard, rolled sleeves, bright kitchen, a pot of soup, a camera on a tripod just in shot.

**Spoken script (29 words, about 13 s of speech inside a 17 s ad):** "Anyway. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

**Must not be lost:** the silence. Two and a half seconds of a frozen host pose, crickets, and the title "The sponsor segment (empty)".

**Fixes applied:** the ad now opens on the silence instead of building up to it, so the beat that only this ad owns lands inside the first 2 seconds. The line "You've left a gap like that" is cut, and "Apply for a sponsor. Free." lands at about second 7.

## 5. Next First (static image only)

**Pitch:** A shelf of a small creator's milestones, each with a handmade trophy, and one stand left empty.

**On the image:** labels "First video", "First comment", "First 1,000 subs", and over the empty stand "First sponsor: ____". Below: "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Free to apply." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor."

**Fix applied:** the clay play-shape trophy is replaced by a plain wooden star, because the play shape imitates another company's mark.

## One visual style for all of them

- **Light:** real daylight. Windows, white walls, colour. Nothing moody.
- **Places:** lived-in creator rooms (kitchen, bedroom studio, home set). A little mess is good.
- **Pace:** hard cuts, no fades. A cut or a new caption at least every 2 seconds.
- **Sound:** every ad has one signature sound (the pop of the cut-off line, the hollow knock of the empty box, the credits tune stopping, crickets). No licensed music.
- **Type:** big, heavy, high-contrast captions, burned in for every spoken line. The blank is always drawn the same way: a thick pen-blue underline.
- **The blank:** "____" in pen blue (`#2B44D6` on light, `#A8B4FF` on dark) appears in every ad and on every end card.
- **End card (same for all):** "Sponsored by ____" with the blank blinking (the logo sits apart from it, never on the same line, so it cannot read as the sponsor), the Apply button, "Free to apply. For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subs.", small "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor.", the real logo, and "Made with AI".
- **Labels:** "AI actor" on any shot with a person. "Pretend" on any acted sponsor moment.
- **Never generated:** words, numbers, logos, screens. All of them are graphics added afterwards.
