# Round 2 concepts (D1 to D6)

Author: Creative Concept Lead. Sources: product-brief.md (sections 2, 9, 10), scorecard.md (sections 1, 2, 5, 7), the L1 section of ideas.md. Nothing from v1.

## What round 2 changes

- Round 1 answered objections. Round 2 sells the thing the creator WANTS: being a sponsored creator, saying it to their audience, being taken seriously while small.
- Every concept dramatises that wish in an obviously unreal way (a daydream, a rehearsal, a blank unbranded object, a spoof format). Nobody ever has a sponsor. No brand, no money, no win.
- None uses L1's device (the rehearsed line cut off before the name). Six different doors into the same wish:

| # | Door into the desire |
|---|---|
| D1 | The ad-read itself: the joy of presenting a sponsor's thing (the thing is a blank box) |
| D2 | The credits: one line missing from a one-person production |
| D3 | The arrival: being announced like someone who matters |
| D4 | The trailer: your bedroom channel treated as a blockbuster |
| D5 | The kit: the empty sponsor spot on the shirt front |
| D6 | The sting: the longing at hearing someone else's sponsor jingle |

## Shared on-screen skeleton (graphics added in the edit, never filmed)

- **0 to 2 s:** a chip, always visible to the end: "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." This makes "sponsor" readable by 2 s and "free to apply" by 3 s with sound off.
- **By 5 s:** "1,000 to 50,000 subs" in big type.
- **Last third:** the answers to objections, brief, always with "to suggest": "A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."
- **End card (3 s):** "Apply" button. "Free to apply. For YouTube channels with 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor."
- Small corner tag "Acted scene. AI-generated." on every concept, in line with the owner's AI label.
- Finished length is about 18 s: 15 s of story plus the 3 s end card.
- One clip is 4 to 15 s, so each ad is built from 3 to 5 short clips with hard cuts.

## D1. The Blank Box

**Pitch:** A small creator does a gleeful, over-the-top ad-read for a plain white box with nothing on it, then tells you the real thing is open to apply for, free.

- **The ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Desire in the creator's words:** "I want to hold something up to my audience and say 'look at this' and have it be a real sponsored segment."

**Outline**

| Time | What we see | What we hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Bright desk, ring light. Creator holds a plain white unbranded box up beside their face with a huge ad-read grin, other hand sweeping across it like a game-show host. | Bright sting. Creator, ad-read voice: "Look at this. Look at this!" | Top: "Your sponsored segment." Chip: "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." Corner tag. |
| 2-5 s | Creator spins the box to show every side. All blank. Camera pushes in, deadpan face, then a delighted nod. | Small fanfare. Narrator: "Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs?" | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs". Tiny label on the box area (added graphic, blank tile): "Brand: ____". |
| 5-10 s | Creator sets the box down, turns to lens, drops the act, warm. | "Apply for a sponsor. It's free." | Big: "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 10-15 s | Same frame, nodding as they speak (no finger counting). | "A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | "A person picks one to suggest." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Sting, then silence. | "Apply". "Free to apply. For 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (33 words):** "Look at this. Look at this! Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why the right viewer stops:** an adult being wildly sincere about a blank box is funny at once, and every small creator knows that exact ad-read grin they would love to be doing for real.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the grin is the feeling they want; the ad then says, to their size, that they can apply for it, for free.
- **Why no competitor could run it unchanged:** the blank unbranded box with the "Brand: ____" tile is a repeatable prop that only makes sense for an offer where the sponsor is the missing piece. A rival with a platform or a database would not make a joke out of the empty brand.
- **Sound-off check (0 to 3 s):** "Your sponsored segment", the chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", a person doing a joyful product show-and-tell of a blank box. A muted viewer reads: sponsor, free, apply, and sees the ad-read pose.
- **Honesty check:** the box is blank on every side and the brand tile reads "Brand: ____", so no sponsor exists on screen. The creator never says they have a sponsor; the pose is a spoof of the ad-read, tagged as acted. The only claim is that you can apply, and the end card says applying doesn't promise one.
- **AI difficulty:** easy. One person, one prop. Risk: AI may print fake logo-like marks on the box. Prompt for "plain matte white box, no text, no marks" and check each frame.
- **Static version:** the creator holding the plain box up, big type "Your sponsored segment goes here", chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply."
- **My score guess:** hook 8, clarity 8, desire 8, difference 8, CTA 8 = **40**.

## D2. The Credits

**Pitch:** The end credits of a one-person YouTube channel roll: filmed by you, edited by you, lit by a desk lamp... and one line missing.

- **The ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Desire in the creator's words:** "I do every job on my channel. I'd love that last line in the credits to have someone else's name in it."

**Outline**

| Time | What we see | What we hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Creator on a bright set filming themselves, looking into the lens with a small grin. Credits scroll up over the frame, film-style. | Bouncy end-credits tune. Deadpan narrator: "Filmed by you." | Credits: "Filmed by: you." Chip: "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." |
| 2-5 s | Four quick hard cuts: carrying a tripod, adjusting a lamp, nodding at a laptop (back to camera, no screen visible), pouring a cold tea. | "Edited by you. Lit by a desk lamp." | Credits: "Edited by: you." / "Lit by: a desk lamp." / "Catering: cold tea." Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs." |
| 5-10 s | Creator sits back, arms folded, and looks up and to the side, toward the credits, as the music thins out. The last credit line is an empty line with a blinking bar. | "One line's missing. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free." | Last line only: "Sponsored by: ____" (blank, blinking). Then big: "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 10-15 s | Same framing, the creator gives a small nod. | "A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | "A person picks one to suggest." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Credits tune ends on a button. | "Apply". "Free to apply. For 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (41 words):** "Filmed by you. Edited by you. Lit by a desk lamp. One line's missing. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why the right viewer stops:** it is their life in four lines: they ARE the whole crew. The credits form is also instantly readable with sound off, since the type is the show.
- **Why they tap Apply:** "Sponsored by: ____" is the only line left to fill. The want is the line, not the form.
- **Why no competitor could run it unchanged:** the missing sponsor line is the offer. A platform or marketplace would never choose to frame itself as one blank line in someone else's credits; it makes the product the missing credit.
- **Sound-off check (0 to 3 s):** credits roll with "Filmed by: you", the chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", a creator in a bright room. A muted viewer reads "sponsor" and "free to apply" and understands the joke from the type alone.
- **Honesty check:** the sponsor line is blank in every frame it appears, and no brand or name ever fills it. Nobody says or implies they have one. It is a credits spoof about the work, not a result.
- **AI difficulty:** easy. The credits are all graphics. The filmed part is one person doing four plain actions. Risk: the laptop. Keep it from behind, no screen in shot, or swap to editing headphones on.
- **Static version:** a credits-style list on a bright colour: "Filmed by: you / Edited by: you / Lit by: a desk lamp / Sponsored by: ____", chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply."
- **My score guess:** hook 8, clarity 9, desire 8, difference 8, CTA 9 = **42**.

## D3. The Red Carpet

**Pitch:** A red carpet rolls across a messy bedroom floor to a creator's desk while a stadium announcer introduces a channel of 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, then asks you to apply for a sponsor.

- **The ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Desire in the creator's words:** "I want to be introduced like I matter, small as I am, and have a sponsor take my channel seriously."

**Outline**

| Time | What we see | What we hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | A bedroom door bursts open. A red carpet unrolls fast across a floor with a laundry pile on it, straight to a desk with a ring light. | Arena hype, drum hit. Announcer: "Ladies and gentlemen!" | Top: "Sponsor offer." Chip: "Free to apply." Big: "INTRODUCING" |
| 2-5 s | Creator in a hoodie walks in down the carpet in slow motion, the ring light snaps on like a spotlight. | Crowd swell. "A channel of one thousand to fifty thousand subscribers!" | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" |
| 5-10 s | Creator sits, gives a small, shy wave to the lens, the crowd roar still going. | "Looking for a sponsor! Make some noise for applying. It's free." | "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 10-15 s | The roar drops. Creator settles in, calm, nodding. Carpet in the background. | Plain voice: "A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | "A person picks to suggest." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Drum hit, then silence. | "Apply". "Free to apply. For 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (38 words):** "Ladies and gentlemen! A channel of one thousand to fifty thousand subscribers! Looking for a sponsor! Make some noise for applying. It's free. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why the right viewer stops:** a red carpet running over a laundry pile is a one-second image of their own room made grand. They also hear their own number said like a title.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the shy wave is the feeling. The want is to be taken seriously while small, and the announcer says the size out loud as if it were enough to be introduced.
- **Why no competitor could run it unchanged:** the introduction is built on the "1,000 to 50,000" range. A rival aimed at big or all creators would not announce a small channel like a headliner.
- **Sound-off check (0 to 3 s):** "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", "INTRODUCING", the red carpet and a creator walking in. The range appears by 3 s.
- **Honesty check:** the only person in the scene is the creator, and the crowd is sound design in an obviously staged fantasy. The announcer introduces the channel and says it is "looking for", never that it has one. No brand, no prize, no win.
- **AI difficulty:** medium. A red carpet over a floor is easy; the unrolling needs a clean cut or a short stylised clip. Keep the walk slow and hands out of frame. Risk: the carpet unroll physics. Fall back to a locked shot with the carpet already laid and a fast push-in.
- **Static version:** a creator seated at a desk at the end of a red carpet across a bedroom floor, big type "Introducing: a channel with 1,000 to 50,000 subs", chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", "Apply".
- **My score guess:** hook 8, clarity 8, desire 8, difference 8, CTA 8 = **40**.

## D4. The Trailer

**Pitch:** A movie-trailer voice treats a bedroom channel like a blockbuster, with title cards that say what is on offer.

- **The ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Desire in the creator's words:** "I want my little channel to be treated as a big deal, with a sponsor, instead of being the thing nobody notices."

**Outline**

| Time | What we see | What we hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Slow push-in on a bedroom desk, a camera on a tripod, ring light off, dust motes in a sunbeam. Bright, not dark. | Deep boom. Trailer voice: "In a world of small channels..." | Card: "A SPONSOR OFFER." Chip: "Free to apply." |
| 2-5 s | Ring light snaps on. A creator grips the camera, looks to the lens with an epic stare. | "...one sponsor offer. Starring you." | Cards, one at a time: "1,000 TO 50,000 SUBS." / "STARRING: YOU." |
| 5-10 s | Fast hard cuts, slow-motion: chair spin, hair flick, lean in to the lens with a grin. | "For one thousand to fifty thousand subscribers. Free to apply." | Cards: "APPLY FOR A SPONSOR." / "FREE TO APPLY." |
| 10-15 s | The trailer drama drops to a plain, friendly voice over a calm shot of the creator. | "A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | Small cards: "A person picks to suggest." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic, trailer "rating" style. | Boom, then silence. | "Apply". "Free to apply. For 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (36 words):** "In a world of small channels, one sponsor offer. Starring you. For one thousand to fifty thousand subscribers. Free to apply. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why the right viewer stops:** the trailer voice over a bedroom desk is a funny mismatch and the cards are loud, bright type that reads without sound.
- **Why they tap Apply:** "Starring: you" turns the channel into the hero of something with a sponsor in it. That is the want: a bigger story for a small channel.
- **Why no competitor could run it unchanged:** "one sponsor offer, for small channels, starring you" only works because the offer is aimed at one size of channel. The trailer is the format; the 1,000 to 50,000 range is the title.
- **Sound-off check (0 to 3 s):** "A SPONSOR OFFER." "Free to apply." and a creator's desk. The range card is at 2 to 5 s.
- **Honesty check:** a trailer is an obvious spoof, and it promotes the offer, not a result. No scene shows a sponsor, a brand, a deal or a win. "Starring you" is the viewer as the one who applies, not a claim that they will get one.
- **AI difficulty:** easy. Cinematic desk shots and one actor are what the models do best. Risk: AI adds fake letters to the desk items. Keep it plain.
- **Static version:** a movie-poster layout: creator at the desk, title "A SPONSOR OFFER", tagline "Starring: you", "1,000 to 50,000 subs. Free to apply."
- **My score guess:** hook 8, clarity 8, desire 8, difference 9, CTA 8 = **41**.

## D5. The Kit

**Pitch:** A small creator pulls on a hoodie with a blank, plain patch on the chest like a team shirt's sponsor spot, stands tall, and tells you that you can apply for one.

- **The ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Desire in the creator's words:** "Footballers have the logo on the front. I want something on my chest that says I'm a real thing."

**Outline**

| Time | What we see | What we hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Close on a hoodie chest with a plain blank white rectangular patch. Two hands smooth it flat. | Stadium-tunnel hum. Creator: "Kit's on." | Top: "Your sponsor spot: empty." Chip: "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." Corner tag. |
| 2-5 s | Creator steps back and stands hands on hips, like walking out onto a pitch, in a bright bedroom studio. | Crowd swell. Narrator: "Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs?" | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" |
| 5-10 s | Creator lifts the camera onto its tripod and turns to the lens, a proud tap on the blank patch with a flat palm. | "Apply for a sponsor. It's free." | "Apply for a sponsor. Free." The patch stays blank. |
| 10-15 s | Same frame, calm, nodding. | "A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | "A person picks one to suggest." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Whistle, then silence. | "Apply". "Free to apply. For 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (29 words):** "Kit's on. Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? Apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks one to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why the right viewer stops:** a hoodie with a conspicuously empty patch is a clear, odd picture. Anyone who knows team shirts sees at once what is missing.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the shirt-front sponsor is the plainest symbol of "being sponsored", and a small creator standing in the kit is the want, made physical.
- **Why no competitor could run it unchanged:** the empty patch as "the spot for your sponsor" fits only an offer about finding that sponsor, and it is shot for the small-creator range.
- **Sound-off check (0 to 3 s):** "Your sponsor spot: empty.", the chip, a blank patch on a chest, a person in a hoodie. Clear.
- **Honesty check:** the patch is blank in every frame and stays blank on the end card. No brand, no logo. The creator wears the kit as a playful spoof, never claims to be sponsored, and it is tagged as acted.
- **AI difficulty:** medium. The patch must stay identical across clips, and it must not get a fake logo. Keep hands simple (smoothing, flat palm). Risk: AI invents text on the patch; regenerate any clip that does.
- **Static version:** chest close-up on the plain patch, big type "Your sponsor spot: empty", chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply."
- **My score guess:** hook 8, clarity 8, desire 9, difference 8, CTA 8 = **41**.

## D6. The Next-Room Sting

**Pitch:** A creator editing alone hears a faint sponsor jingle from another room, goes still with longing, then turns to you: you can apply for a sponsor.

- **The ONE promise:** If your channel has 1,000 to 50,000 subscribers, you can apply for a sponsor, free.
- **Desire in the creator's words:** "Every time I hear that 'this video is sponsored by' sound, I want it to be mine."

**Outline**

| Time | What we see | What we hear | Words on screen (added later) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Creator at a bright desk with headphones round their neck, chin in hand, bored. Head snaps toward the door. | A faint "ding-whoosh" jingle, muffled, from off-screen. No words. | Caption: "♪ sponsor jingle, next room ♪". Chip: "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." |
| 2-5 s | Close on the face: ears up, a long, wistful look at the door. Then a small sigh. | Jingle fades. Narrator: "Hear that? Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs?" | Big: "1,000 to 50,000 subs" |
| 5-10 s | Creator turns from the door to the lens and brightens. | "You can apply for a sponsor. It's free." | "Apply for a sponsor. Free." |
| 10-15 s | Same frame, nodding. | "A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission." | "A person picks to suggest." / "You can say no." / "No deal, no commission." |
| End card (3 s) | Clean graphic. | Jingle ends on a button. | "Apply". "Free to apply. For 1,000 to 50,000 subs." Small: "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor." |

**Spoken script (32 words):** "Hear that? Got one thousand to fifty thousand subs? You can apply for a sponsor. It's free. A person picks a sponsor to suggest. You can say no. No deal, no commission."

- **Why the right viewer stops:** the longing face at a sound they know is the want, shown at once. It is a tiny, honest moment of envy.
- **Why they tap Apply:** the wish gets a next step, said to their size, at once.
- **Why no competitor could run it unchanged:** a jingle for "someone else's sponsor" and a viewer who wants it fits only a sponsor offer for small creators. The sound can be reused as a recognisable audio sting across the whole campaign.
- **Sound-off check (0 to 3 s):** the caption "♪ sponsor jingle, next room ♪", the chip, a creator staring at a door. This is the weakest muted read of the six; the caption carries it.
- **Honesty check:** the jingle is wordless, from another room, and nobody on screen has a sponsor. The creator is the one wishing. No brand, no deal, no win.
- **AI difficulty:** easy. One person, one room, a head turn and a look. Risk: the look must read as longing, not worry. Test the clip before committing.
- **Static version:** the creator looking wistfully at a door, big type "That's the sound you want?", chip "Sponsor offer. Free to apply.", "1,000 to 50,000 subs? Apply."
- **My score guess:** hook 8, clarity 7, desire 8, difference 8, CTA 8 = **39**.

## Cut and replace log

- First draft D6 was "The Bumper" (a creator happily interrupted by a "sponsored segment" swoosh graphic). Scored 37 by my own count: it needed the creator to say "sponsored segment", which reads as already having one, and it was mostly a graphic with little to feel. Cut.
- Replaced by D6 "The Next-Room Sting", which dramatises only the wish (hearing, not having).
- Also discarded before drafting: "The Empty Chair" (a reserved seat beside the creator). It implies a sponsor is on the way, which breaks the honesty rule.
- D6 is still my weakest at 39. I would defend it for its different sensory door (sound and longing), and run it as a test variant, not a lead.

## My own ranking

1. **D2 The Credits (42):** clearest, easiest to make, strongest muted read.
2. **D4 The Trailer (41) and D5 The Kit (41):** tied. Trailer is the most ownable format; Kit is the strongest single image of "being sponsored".
3. D3 and D1 (40), then D6 (39).
