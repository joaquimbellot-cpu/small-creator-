## 1. Verdict

1. Two of the 14 new concepts survive: L3 Missing Sponsor (41) and D2 The Credits (40, on the bare line). With L1 (41) that is three survivors. Twelve of 14 die.
2. The authors' own guesses (D and E sets, 11 concepts) average 40.5. My totals for the same 11 average 35.8. The gap is mostly desire: a gag about a missing sponsor is funnier than the want it points at.
3. Kills: D3 and D4 fail bland test line 4 (an announcer or trailer voice over a small bedroom is the default ad voice); D6 fails line 2 and is the weakest with sound off; the other nine die on score (E4 39, L4 38, D1 38, D5 37, E3 36, E1 36, L5 36, E2 35, E5 34).
4. Round 2 did fix round 1's main fault: every concept now has "sponsor" in frame one and sells wanting, not an objection. It also converged: 12 of the 14 are the same idea, "a place where the sponsor should be is empty", told with a different prop (section 6). That is a test pool of about four hooks, not fourteen.
5. Open risks on the survivors: L3 is the riskiest to generate (hands presenting an invisible object, which video models fill in with a made-up product) and carries a spoken "Huge thanks to today's sponsor"; D2 is one point from dying; L1 still has its three round 1 fixes outstanding.

## 3. Concept by concept

**L3 Missing Sponsor (41).**
- Strongest: "Sponsor segment. Missing: the sponsor." is on screen in frame one, so the gap and the offer are readable at once. The made-up read ("Comes in three colours") is a sharp, true parody of ad copy aimed at nothing.
- Weakest: it is L1's device again (a sponsor read with the name missing). Desire is 8, not 9: it says "I know how I'd do it", where L1 is the exact sentence they imagine.
- Honesty: medium. A fast viewer with sound on hears "Huge thanks to today's sponsor" before the stamp registers. "You can say no to any of them" implies several sponsors waiting. The brief wording is "turn down any sponsor".
- Production: high. The whole open is hands presenting an object that does not exist; video models fill empty hands with an object, and "tap it, hold it to the light" is worse. Open hands also risk finger errors.
- Sound-off: strong. The stamp, title and caption carry it. "Free to apply" arrives at 2 s and the subscriber range only at 8 s, which is late.

**D2 The Credits (40).**
- Strongest: "Sponsored by: ____" is nearly L1's sentence, and the credits are made of type, so it reads fully muted. Easiest of the survivors to make.
- Weakest: the hook is a list. Seconds 0 to 2 are "Filmed by: you" and the chip; the blank line does not appear until 5 to 10 s. The only "sponsor" in the first 2 s is a small chip.
- Honesty: low. The line is blank in every frame and nobody fills it.
- Production: low. Credits are graphics. Risks: the cold tea pour (liquid), carrying a tripod (hands), the laptop must not show a screen.
- Sound-off: excellent, the best of the set.

**E4 The Gap (39).**
- Strongest: the dead-air beat after "the sponsor segment" is the most unusual moment in the pool and the one idea no competitor can copy. Desire 8: it is the slot in the viewer's own video.
- Weakest: the beat arrives at 4.4 s, after the scroll decision. Seconds 0 to 2 are a man stirring soup, which looks like any cooking video. "You've left a gap like that" is clumsy and presumes a habit.
- Honesty: low. The segment is named, then shown empty with a "(empty)" title.
- Production: medium. Holding a pose in silence can look like a clip error; keep the wrist and wall free of marks.
- Sound-off: fine, but silence is the gag, so the "[crickets]" caption has to do the work.

**L4 Next First (38).**
- Strongest: no presenter, no spoof voice, and "First video / First comment / First 1,000 subs / First sponsor: ____" is the calmest and most honest framing of the want. It makes a static of its own.
- Weakest: the first 2 s is a pan across a shelf, which is soft, and "First sponsor: ____" is a small label on a moving shot. A shelf with no person has the weakest CTA.
- Honesty: low. The stand stays empty. Mild risk: "the next first" frames a sponsor as inevitable milestone, not an application.
- Production: low. But the "clay play-shape" trophy imitates YouTube's play button mark, which breaks section 9 (no YouTube logo). Replace it.
- Sound-off: fully readable, if the label is large.

**D1 The Blank Box (38).**
- Strongest: a grown-up doing a delighted ad read for a plain white box is an odd, instant picture. Prop is safe to generate.
- Weakest: it is L3 with a visible prop. "Your sponsored segment" as the headline is the main flaw: it reads as the creator already having one, and it says nothing.
- Honesty: medium, for that headline and the "Brand: ____" tile on a box that could pick up a stray mark.
- Production: low. Risk: the model prints logo-like marks on the box.
- Sound-off: works.

**D5 The Kit (37).**
- Strongest: a blank patch on a hoodie is a clear, odd picture.
- Weakest: it borrows football. YouTubers do not wear sponsors on their chests; the real want is a segment in a video. "Kit's on" and the pitch walk-out confuse the 5-second read.
- Honesty: medium. The proud tap on the patch reads as pride in something. If the model puts a faint logo on the patch, the ad becomes a sponsored kit.
- Production: medium. The patch must stay identical and blank across clips.
- Sound-off: works.

**E3 Banana Phone (36).**
- Strongest: the best scroll-stopper in the pool. A woman answering a banana in a posh voice stops a thumb in a second.
- Weakest: the want has drifted. "Tuesday. Also Tuesday." is about looking busy, not about a sponsor. "Banana as phone" is a stock gag.
- Honesty: low. The banana is an unmistakable pretend and the diary is blank.
- Production: medium. Diary page-flips and a pen tap, plus the blank pages must stay blank. The author's "low" is optimistic.
- Sound-off: works, with the "Pretend" caption.

**E1 Where It Would Go (36).**
- Strongest: the kettle line is a real laugh and the empty wall reads at once.
- Weakest: a sponsor on a workshop wall is not what a sponsored YouTube segment is. The want is misplaced, and the same idea as D5 and L4.
- Honesty: low. Accuracy note: it implies the sponsor appears on the set.
- Production: medium. One kettle interaction, keep the shed identical.
- Sound-off: works.

**L5 Not 100K (36).**
- Strongest: shoving "100K" off the screen is a strong first second with a number in it. It names the viewer's belief and flips it.
- Weakest: desire 6. It is permission, not wanting, the finding that capped L2 at 34 in round 1. It is round 1's height cluster again.
- Honesty: low. "Sponsor at 100K?" is a question about the viewer's own assumption.
- Production: high. The actor mimes pushing and catching objects that are added later. The scale and timing between a person and a graphic is the miming problem on the risk list.
- Sound-off: works.

**E2 Mouthing Along (35).**
- Strongest: the tomato held like a product is the one good image.
- Weakest: "You mouth along to sponsor reads" accuses the viewer of a habit most do not have, and the offer arrives at 9.4 s. The tag "The tomato isn't sponsored" draws attention to the thing it denies.
- Honesty: medium. A dramatised ad read, saved only by the tag.
- Production: high. Lips that mouth without speaking, the laptop kept unseen, hands.
- Sound-off: the mouthing reads, the point is slow.

**D3 The Red Carpet (34).**
- Strongest: a carpet unrolling across a laundry pile is a one-second picture of the viewer's own room made grand.
- Weakest: "Ladies and gentlemen!" and a crowd roar is a stock device, so it fails line 4. It also gives a status fantasy, not a sponsor.
- Honesty: low. The creator is introduced, never shown with a sponsor.
- Production: high. Carpet unrolling physics.
- Sound-off: the picture works. The announcer is lost.

**E5 Sponsor-Ready Shelf (34).**
- Strongest: the plain true habit of tidying for the camera.
- Weakest: "Sponsor-ready" suggests a test of how tidy you are, which is not how the offer works (a person looks at the channel). It is more tidying than sponsor.
- Honesty: medium. "As if a sponsor's looking" and "sponsor-ready" read like eligibility and approval.
- Production: high. Throwing laundry and a pizza box. Hard cuts are the only fix.
- Sound-off: works.

**D4 The Trailer (33).**
- Strongest: bright cards that read muted, easy to generate.
- Weakest: "In a world of..." is the most parodied voice in advertising, and "Starring: you" gives a hero fantasy, not a sponsor. Fails line 4.
- Honesty: low. A spoof, no sponsor shown. "Starring you" is a mild promise.
- Production: low.
- Sound-off: works.

**D6 The Next-Room Sting (32).**
- Strongest: the wistful face at someone else's jingle is a true feeling.
- Weakest: the sound is the idea. A muted viewer sees a person looking at a door. Why a sponsor jingle is in the next room is unexplained.
- Honesty: low. Nobody has a sponsor.
- Production: low. Risk: the look reads as worry, not longing.
- Sound-off: the weakest in the pool, as the author admits.

## 4. Concepts at 36 to 39: the one change

**E4 The Gap (39).** Open on the silence: start frozen in the host pose with the title "The sponsor segment (empty)" (his own alternative opening 1), so the one beat only this ad owns lands inside 0 to 2 s. Hook 7 to 8 gives 40. Once the open already says "sponsor segment, empty", the clumsy "You've left a gap like that" line can go and "Apply for a sponsor. Free." lands at about 7 s: clarity 7 to 8 gives 41.

**L4 Next First (38).** Cannot be saved as a video. The need is two points, and the weakness is structural: no person, so no hook motion and a thin CTA. As a static or carousel (one frame carries the hook) it is already the best static in the pool. Replace the clay play-shape trophy in either case.

**D1 The Blank Box (38).** Cannot be saved as its own ad: it is L3 with a prop. Its worth is production: a plain white box is the safe stand-in for L3's invisible product (see section 5).

**D5 The Kit (37).** Cannot be saved. The football metaphor is the problem and is the concept. Moving the blank spot to where a YouTuber really has one (the video, the credits) turns it into D2 or E4.

**E3 Banana Phone (36).** Cannot be saved as a stand-alone. The desire has drifted to "busy diary". Keep the banana snatch as a 2-second opening hook variant on a body that says the offer plainly (section 6).

**E1 Where It Would Go (36).** Cannot be saved. A sponsor on a wall is the wrong product picture, and E4 and D2 do the same job better.

**L5 Not 100K (36).** Cannot be saved. Desire is capped at 6 because it is permission, not wanting. It is usable only as an objection-led hook variant (section 6), and the mime must be cut for a text-only version.

## 5. Survivors: what must not be lost, what to fix before sketching

**L1 The Line (41).**
- Must not be lost: the line is never finished. The name is cut off every time, including the last shot; no brand, no "got it" moment.
- Fix before sketching (carried from round 1): replace "A person picks one for your channel" with "A person picks a sponsor to suggest"; burn "This video is sponsored by ____" in as a caption for sound-off; move the 1,000 to 50,000 chip to seconds 2 to 3. Add the "Pretend" tag the E set uses.

**L3 Missing Sponsor (41).**
- Must not be lost: empty hands plus the stamp "Missing: the sponsor" in frame one, before the voice speaks. The read is pure nonsense aimed at nothing, and the look down at the empty hands is the beat.
- Fix before sketching: (1) put the stamp on screen before "Huge thanks to today's sponsor" is heard, and add a "Pretend" tag, so a sound-on viewer cannot take it as a real sponsor. (2) Change "You can say no to any of them" to "You can say no." (3) Move "1,000 to 50,000 subs" from 8 s to 4 to 6 s. (4) Production: do not generate hands presenting nothing. Either use D1's plain white blank box (no text, no marks) as the stand-in with the stamp unchanged, or build the open from a locked medium shot with hands below the chest and cut on the look down. Check every frame for an invented object.

**D2 The Credits (40).**
- Must not be lost: the last credit line stays blank in every frame, including the end card, and no name ever fills it. The credits must stay type, readable muted.
- Fix before sketching: (1) bring "Sponsored by: ____" forward to about 3 to 4 s by cutting to three lines ("Filmed by: you / Edited by: you / Sponsored by: ____"). The hook is its weak point and this is the fix. (2) Make "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." large for 0 to 2 s; it is the only "sponsor" in that window. (3) Replace the cold-tea pour with a mug on the desk (liquid physics). (4) Laptop back to camera or headphones on, no screen in shot.

- **Cluster C: loud hype over a small bedroom.** D3, D4 (E3's pretend call is a gentler cousin). All three fail or nearly fail on stock. Strongest: D3 (34) and E3 (36), but only E3's banana snatch is worth keeping, as a 2-second opening variant. Drop D3 and D4.
- **Cluster D: the private longing habit.** E2, E5, D6. None survive and none adds a hook the survivors lack. Drop all three; only E2's tomato is reusable, and it is not needed.
- **Cluster E: size and permission.** L5 (and round 1's L2 and U3). Strongest: L5 as a hook, but desire stays at 6. It adds the only "too small" objection hook in the pool, as a text-only 6-second variant, never a lead.

### The Line (41, unchanged)
- Fixes applied properly: yes. "A person picks a sponsor to suggest", the burned-in caption, the range chip at second 2, and a "Pretend" tag are all in the script.
- Score change: none. Nothing was weak that a fix could remove. The first 6 seconds still explain little except through the caption.
- Broke nothing.
- Storyboard must design around: the cut-off line is the thing a fast viewer can read as "this person has a sponsor". The creator must look like someone rehearsing (shaking out shoulders, a restart, a breath), never pleased or finished. The "Pretend" tag must be visible on the first take, not only later.

### Missing Sponsor (40, down 1 from 41)
- Fixes applied properly: yes. The stamp and tag come first, "any of them" is gone, the range is at 4 to 6 s, and the box is plain with no text.
- Score change: difference 8 to 7. The invisible product was a stranger picture. A held white box is the D1 prop, which I scored 7 on difference. The added beat (turning the box over for a name and finding none) is good and keeps hook and desire where they were. It removes the AI hand problem but is not a score gain.
- What the fix broke: a visible object in the hands is closer to a real sponsor read than empty hands were. Nothing else has changed.
- Storyboard must design around: (1) the thanks line must not be heard before the stamp and "Pretend" tag are on screen. Hold the voice for about half a second, or open with the box and stamp in frame and no sound, so a sound-on viewer does not hear "Huge thanks to today's sponsor" first. (2) The box must read as a prop: one plain matte colour, no text, no marks, no brand-like shape, no ribbon or tape that suggests a package from a company. Check every frame, since models add marks. (3) "Comes in three colours" is spoken about a single-colour box on purpose. The caption of that line must sit on the same screen as the stamp.

### The Credits (40, unchanged)
- Fixes applied properly: yes. Three lines, "Sponsored by: ____" by 3 to 4 s, a large "Sponsor offer. Free to apply." for 0 to 2 s, a mug on the desk, and no screen.
- Score change: none. The chip and the earlier blank fix the clarity and the first 2 seconds, but the hook itself is still a deadpan "Filmed by: you" over a creator doing chores, so hook stays 7. Cutting "Lit by: a desk lamp" and the tea took a little of the humour; desire stays 8.
- Watch: the spoken "Sponsored by." followed by "One line's missing" now uses L1's cut-off device. It does not change the score against competitors, but it overlaps The Line in the same test set.
- Storyboard must design around: the blank line stays blank in every frame, with no name or logo near it. The credits voice is a narrator, not the creator, so nobody "says" a sponsor.

### The Gap (40, up 1 from 39)
- Fixes applied properly: yes. The ad opens on the silence, "You've left a gap like that" is cut, and the offer lands at about 7 s.
- Score change: clarity 7 to 8. The clumsy line is gone, and "sponsor segment (empty)" plus the range is readable by about 3 s. Hook stays 7, not 8 as I projected. The beat now sits in 0 to 2 s, but a motionless man with no sound is exactly what a muted viewer reads as a stalled video. The beat only works if it is clearly a joke.
- What the fix broke: the spoken setup ("And now, the sponsor segment") is gone, so the joke now relies on the title alone.
- Storyboard must design around: the freeze must not be a frozen frame. Keep small live motion (steam from the soup, a slow blink, eyes moving to the lens, a glance at a bare wrist) and put the "[crickets]" caption in frame one. The "Free to apply" chip and the range band must stay in this ad; selection.md does not restate them. The blank wall must be plain.

### Risks across all four
- **The end card:** "Sponsored by ____" with the blinking blank and the real logo both on one card. If the logo sits next to or under the blank, a fast viewer reads it as the blank filled in: "Sponsored by [company]". Keep the logo small, away from the blank, and below the Apply button. The blank must be the only thing on its line.
- **Label clutter:** "AI actor", "Pretend", the stamp, the chip and the captions can crowd a 9:16 frame. The "Pretend" tag and the stamp take priority over the chip in the first 2 seconds.

### Next First, static for the Meta feed
**Yes, acceptable.** The play shape is gone (a wooden star), the stand stays empty, nobody is shown with a sponsor, and "Applying doesn't promise a sponsor" is on the image. Two conditions: use the exact words "Free to apply" instead of "Apply free", and make the small print legible at feed size (it is the line that carries the honesty). Add the "Made with AI" label.
